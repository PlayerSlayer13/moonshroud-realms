## The Preceptory and the Faith

<details id="grand-preceptory">
<summary><strong>Grand Preceptory of Saint Eccles</strong> — the great sandstone sanctuary south of the village</summary>

<p>The Grand Preceptory of <a href="{{< relref "the-gods#saint-eccles" >}}">Saint Eccles</a> is the principal house of the Church of Saint Eccles in Elderland and the foremost training center for its clerics and knightly orders. The sandstone complex rises from the southern approach to Pistil Point, about 660 feet long, 250 feet wide, and nearly 120 feet high, one of the largest religious monuments in the kingdom. Its towers and broad façade can be seen for miles across the Sea of Zephyrys and the western Plains of Bounty, and they have become symbols of guidance, duty, and safe passage.</p>

<p>It is not a parish temple but a place of learning, discipline, and service. Acolytes, clerics, squires, paladins, and temple wardens spend years in study, meditation, martial training, and service before taking up duties across the realm. Respectful visitors are welcome on the grounds, though most of the complex is reserved for the Church and its students. The Great Hall at its heart opens to the public only for major holy observances, festivals, and ceremonies, when pilgrims and villagers gather for worship, investitures, remembrances, and communal feasts.</p>

<p>The sanctuary is supported by ancillary buildings and training grounds that make it a self-contained religious community. Stables hold riding horses, patrol mounts, wagons, and the steeds of the Order of the Open Road, and workshops, kitchens, stores, and residences serve the hundreds who live and train here. A reception house greets pilgrims, recruits, and official visitors, and a private pier and boathouse keep a longboat and smaller craft for ferrying passengers from anchored ships and for coastal rescues. Inside the main building, cloisters, libraries, refectories, dormitories, classrooms, armories, and meditation halls fill the wings and lower levels.</p>

</details>

<details id="training-grounds">
<summary><strong>Training Grounds</strong> — where the faithful of Saint Eccles drill</summary>

<p>East of the Grand Preceptory, these wide training grounds are where the faithful of Saint Eccles sharpen the skills they need to protect travelers. From dawn to dusk the fields ring with shouted commands, clashing practice weapons, and the rhythm of marching formations as squires, clerics, temple wardens, and paladins prepare for lives of service on the kingdom's roads. There are open fields for mounted maneuvers and formation drills, ranges for archery, crossbows, and siege engines such as the ballistae used to defend fortified positions and escort caravans, and obstacle courses, sparring circles, and endurance routes.</p>

<p>A stout timber equipment hall stores training weapons, armor, tack, and siege gear. Large canvas pavilions go up through the year for major exercises, seasonal musters, and visiting companies of the Order of the Open Road, and they also shelter instruction, meals, medical care, and rest. The grounds are mainly for the Preceptory's residents, but visitors can often watch from designated areas, and pilgrims on the nearby roads often stop to see columns of paladins drilling under fluttering banners.</p>

</details>

<details id="shrine-of-the-humble-virtues">
<summary><strong>Shrine of the Humble Virtues</strong> — the village's red granite shrine</summary>

<p>The Shrine of the Humble Virtues is the largest building inside Pistil Point's walls and the village's spiritual heart. It is built of warm red granite, and its broad halls are devoted to no single Eternal but to the shared ideals of the Resonant Virtues, the universal principles recognized throughout the Moonshroud pantheon. Individual chapels, alcoves, and devotional spaces let worshippers honor the Eternal, Ascendant, or local saint of their choice, a reminder that all the faithful ultimately walk a common path.</p>

<p>The shrine keeps no permanent priesthood for every faith. Its ministry is coordinated through an ecclesiastical council in Annapore, which sends visiting clergy to Pistil Point on rotations of about two months, planned well ahead as part of the council's care for shrines throughout western Algar. The result is a steady succession of priests, clerics, and scholars whose traditions enrich village life. The Grand Preceptory plays a supporting role, since Ecclesian acolytes are assigned to the shrine as part of their education, helping with daily worship, ceremonies, charitable work, and pastoral care and learning the rites and teachings of the wider pantheon. The arrangement reflects the Ecclesian belief that those who would guide travelers must first understand the many roads others choose.</p>

<p>The shrine is also one of the principal gathering places in the village. Weddings, funerals, civic blessings, harvest thanksgivings, and prayers for departing sailors all take place under its vaulted roof, and villagers often seek guidance more than answers, so a conversation begun before one altar may well end before another.</p>

</details>

## The Crown and the Reeve

<details id="royal-muster-hall">
<summary><strong>Royal Muster Hall</strong> — the Crown's patrol headquarters at the crossroads</summary>

<p>The Royal Muster Hall stands at the junction of the coastal royal road and the road leading north into Pistil Point, the Crown's administrative and military presence beyond the village walls. It is built of sturdy dressed stone, with a defensible gatehouse, stables, and an enclosed yard, and serves as headquarters for the mounted patrols that keep the surrounding royal roads safe for merchants, pilgrims, and local travelers. Soldiers are also quartered inside the village, but patrols start and finish their circuits here, changing horses, drawing supplies, filing reports, and collecting official notices before riding out across the western Plains of Bounty or southeast toward Ormith and the Duchy of Rhundel.</p>

<p>The hall also holds offices where royal officials inspect commercial cargoes, collect tolls and duties, register official caravans, and authenticate travel warrants or sealed dispatches. Its position reflects practical importance more than any need for defense, since the Grand Preceptory is close by and few threats linger long. Its purpose is to keep the Crown visible on one of Algar's principal coastal routes.</p>

</details>

<details id="bastelin-manor">
<summary><strong>Bastelin Manor</strong> — the ancestral seat of the Royal Reeve</summary>

<p>Bastelin Manor stands on the southern road just beyond the crossroads and has been the ancestral seat of House Bastelin for more than a century and a half. The family first settled the peninsula as orchardists and turned its fertile soil into the thriving apple groves that built Queensland's reputation for ciders and preserves. Their stewardship and service to the Crown earned them noble status and the office of Royal Reeve, which the family has held across generations.</p>

<p>The estate is the reeve's residence and the administrative heart of the surrounding district, ringed by tenant farms, orchards, granaries, workshops, and well-kept stables. Visiting officials, guild representatives, and local petitioners are received in its halls. The current lord, Lord Bavar Bastelin, is a stoneheart mustelin and the Crown's principal representative in Pistil Point and the district around it. The villagers, the Grand Preceptory, and the Queensland Farmers' Union all respect him, and he is regarded as a fair and practical steward whose measured judgment has brought stability to the peninsula for many years.</p>

</details>

<details id="alghorand-house">
<summary><strong>Alghorand House</strong> — the Royal Reeve's offices on the village square</summary>

<p>Alghorand House is named for the ancient landmass of Alghorand, from which Algar takes its name, and serves as the official residence and administrative offices of the Royal Reeve of Pistil Point. The reeve may keep a private home elsewhere, but all official Crown business in the district is conducted from this distinguished stone building overlooking the village square. The ground floor holds public offices, record rooms, a hearing chamber, and secure archives where petitions are received, licenses issued, and taxes and duties recorded. Above are apartments for the reeve and well-appointed guest chambers for visiting magistrates, military officers, royal inspectors, and members of the royal household on official business.</p>

<p>A Royal Adventuring Society briefing officer from Annapore is posted here from time to time to coordinate Crown-sponsored expeditions, deliver sealed assignments, and debrief members back from official missions. The officer is not always present, but Society members can reliably leave reports, letters, or requests with the reeve's staff, who keep secure contact with the regional headquarters. It is quieter than the Exchange, but it is the administrative heart of the peninsula, where the Crown, the district's landholders, and the wider realm meet.</p>

</details>

<details id="crown-barracks">
<summary><strong>Crown Barracks</strong> — headquarters of the Crown Infantry on the peninsula</summary>

<p>The Crown Barracks occupy the northern part of Pistil Point's Royal Compound and are the headquarters of the Algarian Crown Infantry on the peninsula. The soldiers stationed here patrol the district's royal roads, man the Main Gates and Gatewatch Keep, collect excise duties, escort officials, and respond to emergencies across the region. The multi-story building holds barrack rooms, kitchens, mess halls, armories, supply stores, officers' quarters, offices, and a modest infirmary, and nearby stables house the patrol horses and transport wagons. The commander's residence stands close by in the compound.</p>

<p>The garrison has no training ground of its own, but standing arrangements with the Grand Preceptory let Crown troops drill with Ecclesian paladins and fighting clerics on the neighboring fields. Together with Alghorand House, the commander's residence, and the stables, the barracks make up the Royal Compound, the center of the Crown's presence in Pistil Point, and in an emergency it can expand to house reinforcements from Annapore or Ohlmith.</p>

</details>

## Walls and Gates

<details id="seawall">
<summary><strong>Seawall</strong> — the ancient wall across the peninsula's neck</summary>

<p>The seawall crosses the narrowest part of the peninsula west of Pistil Point and has protected the settlement from wind, tide, and storm for centuries. It is built of immense fitted blocks of stone and has been repaired and strengthened many times, though the local masons proudly insist that many of the original stones still sit where they were first laid. It shields the vulnerable neck of land that joins the village to the mainland.</p>

<p>Tradition says the wall was designed by Sir Valory Eccles on his travels along the western coast, when tides and seasonal storms were eroding the peninsula and threatening to turn it into an island. Rather than simply resist the sea, he is said to have studied the coastline and placed the wall so precisely that it redirected the currents and broke the force of the waves, and the erosion stopped. Whether that was engineering, divine guidance, or legend, the peninsula has endured. Residents stroll the wall to watch the surf and the sunsets, and pilgrims pause on it to reflect on the patience of Saint Eccles. To the village it is a reminder that strength lies in understanding nature well enough to work with it.</p>

</details>

<details id="main-gates">
<summary><strong>Main Gates</strong> — the Crown-manned southern entrance</summary>

<p>The southern entrance to Pistil Point continues the Ohlmith Trail, just north of the junction with the Shore Road, and is the village's busiest and most important gateway. Twin stone and wood gatehouses flank the entrance, where soldiers of the Algarian Crown Infantry inspect travelers, levy tolls and excise duties where required, and secure the royal burg. A small corps of local volunteer watchmen assists them in the watchtowers, many of them retired gullfolk mariners whose sharp eyes and knowledge of the district make them valuable lookouts.</p>

<p>Just beyond the walls beside the highway stands Gatewatch Keep, a fortified Crown stronghouse that serves as the military headquarters for the village's landward approaches. It can house a full squadron of infantry and cavalry when needed, and normally holds the gate garrison with its stores, stables, armory, and holding cells. It also contains the Crown's customs and excise offices, secure records, and quarters for a commissioned officer, so that senior officers, royal inspectors, magistrates, or visiting dignitaries can conduct Crown business without disturbing the daily work of Alghorand House.</p>

</details>

<details id="west-gate">
<summary><strong>West Gate</strong> — the fishers' gate to the docks</summary>

<p>The West Gate opens toward the fisheries and docks along the western shore and is the busiest of Pistil Point's local gates, the lifeblood of its fishing trade. Before dawn crews stream through toward waiting boats, and at dusk the gate fills again with fishers, dockworkers, and laborers returning. It is narrower than the Main Gates but wide enough for wagons carrying barrels, nets, timber, and the day's catch.</p>

<p>Trusted local volunteers keep it, not Crown soldiers, and most know the fishermen, merchants, and dockworkers by name. Their duties are as much about helping the community as about security. Strangers who arrive here by mistake are politely sent to the Main Gates, while those leaving seldom get more than a friendly farewell. The watch is as likely to help unload a cart of fish or secure a storm-battered boat as to challenge a stranger, but in severe weather the gate becomes a hive of activity as volunteers, fishers, and Crown soldiers work together to protect the harbor and help vessels coming in from the sea.</p>

</details>

<details id="east-gate">
<summary><strong>East Gate</strong> — the quiet gate to the bay shore</summary>

<p>The East Gate is the quietest of Pistil Point's entrances and opens onto the sheltered shores of Nereth Bay and the narrow shoreline path along the peninsula's eastern coast. It is wide enough for wagons at the autumn harvest, but it is used mostly by local farmers, orchard workers, and villagers walking to the beaches or tending small boats. Local volunteers watch it, and strangers are politely directed to the Main Gates if they wish to enter. Most days the gatekeeper's work is greeting familiar faces and watching the weather drift across the bay.</p>

<p>In recent decades the sheltered beaches beyond the gate have slowly come back to life after the devastation that struck Nereth Bay generations ago. The waters nearer the mouth of the Gold River are still unsafe, but the southern reaches now support seabirds, shellfish, and small fish, and in warm months villagers fish, collect driftwood and shells, or enjoy the sea air under the silhouette of the Grand Preceptory above the walls.</p>

</details>

<details id="north-gate">
<summary><strong>North Gate</strong> — the gate to the smithy, lighthouse, and spit</summary>

<p>The North Gate is similar in design and operation to the East and West Gates and serves the workshops, shoreline, and landmarks of the northern reaches. Local volunteers man it, and it sees a steady but irregular flow of farmers, craftsmen, fishermen, and visitors through the day, not the rushes of morning and evening that mark the West Gate. Beyond it the road leads to one of the village's busiest smithies, where tools, horseshoes, anchors, nails, and fishing hardware are forged for the district, and farther on to the lighthouse, several private piers, and the sandbar linked to the mainland by a rope bridge, a favorite fair-weather outing. Pilgrims also use the route for a quiet walk away from the bustle, and so do travelers who want the sea views before moving on.</p>

<p>It is rarely crowded, yet it is one of the village's most frequently used entrances. The watchkeepers know many of those who pass by name and are often the first to notice when a stranger appears or a familiar face fails to return before nightfall.</p>

</details>

## Inns, Shops, and Markets

<details id="sea-cider-inn">
<summary><strong>Sea Cider Inn</strong> — the gullfolk inn east of the crossroads</summary>

<p>The Sea Cider Inn sits just east of the crossroads where the coastal royal road meets the road to Pistil Point, a welcome stop before the village gates. It has a broad common room, comfortable guest chambers, and a spacious courtyard, and it is popular with merchants, pilgrims, and holidaymakers visiting the recovering coastline or the Grand Preceptory. Travelers who want only a short stay often choose its quieter rooms over going on into the village.</p>

<p>The gullfolk Brinefeather family has owned and run the inn for generations, and it is best known for its house-made sea cider, pressed from local apples and fermented to a closely guarded family recipe. Hearty coastal fare and local ales add to its reputation. The atmosphere is lively but respectable. Clerics, paladins, squires, and temple wardens gather here after long days of training, sharing tables with villagers, farmers, and passing travelers, and Crown soldiers often join them after road patrols. There are friendly rivalries, but the respect between the Crown's servants and the faithful of Saint Eccles is plain to see.</p>

</details>

<details id="pink-pearl-inn">
<summary><strong>The Pink Pearl Inn</strong> — the village's common room</summary>

<p>The Pink Pearl Inn stands near the heart of Pistil Point, a welcoming three-story wattle-and-daub building owned and run by the gullfolk innkeeper Gohlo Konono. Nearly everyone in the village knows Gohlo, who is as famous for remembering a guest's favorite drink as for passing along the latest local gossip with a warm smile and a hearty laugh. The tavern is lively all year, serving fresh seafood, orchard produce, and locally brewed ciders and ales in generous portions at fair prices. Visitors are welcome, but the Pink Pearl is first of all the village's common room, where fishermen end their working day, farmers swap news from the orchards, and Crown soldiers have a quiet meal off duty.</p>

<p>The guest chambers are comfortable but seldom full outside the busiest seasons. At harvest festivals, major holy days at the Preceptory, or large coastal gatherings every bed is claimed quickly and travelers may end up sharing rooms or looking elsewhere. A neatly landscaped yard shaded by mature trees surrounds the inn, and its spacious stable takes horses, wagons, and the occasional pack animal.</p>

</details>

<details id="north-point-sundry">
<summary><strong>North Point Sundry</strong> — the village general store</summary>

<p>North Point Sundry is part general store, part outfitter, and part chandlery, and it supplies much of the everyday equipment the people of Pistil Point need. The riverheart mustelin Arno Graybrook owns it, a friendly and methodical man, and the shop carries a little of everything, from household necessities to the practical gear fishers, pilgrims, and travelers need. Weapons, armor, and fresh provisions are generally bought elsewhere, but the shelves and bins overflow with ropes, nets, tackle, lanterns, candles, oil, tents, bedrolls, sacks, blankets, cookware, nails, tools, twine, flour, beans, dried fruit, rations, and much else. Local farmers come for seed sacks and repair materials, and visiting adventurers often buy forgotten supplies before setting out on the roads.</p>

<p>Arno's storeroom is something of a local legend. Despite the apparent clutter, he can produce exactly the item a customer needs with a thoughtful nod and a quick disappearance behind the shelves. When he does not stock something, he can almost always send the customer to the right craftsman, guild, or neighbor in Pistil Point who does.</p>

</details>

<details id="queensland-exchange">
<summary><strong>Queensland Exchange</strong> — the commercial heart of the peninsula</summary>

<p>The Queensland Exchange is the second-largest building inside the village walls, after the Shrine of the Humble Virtues, and the commercial heart of the peninsula. Its spacious timber-framed market hall holds a permanent indoor marketplace where fishers, orchardists, farmers, bakers, brewers, and artisans sell fresh seafood, orchard fruit, breads, preserves, ciders, cheeses, flowers, textiles, and many other local goods. The market is busiest at harvests and festivals but runs all year, and it is one of the liveliest gathering places in the village.</p>

<p>Offices, meeting rooms, and storehouses at the rear are the permanent home of the Queensland Farmers' Union and regional representatives of the Merchants' Exchange, the Brewers' & Grocers' Compact, the Dockworkers' Guild, the Mariners' & Rivermen's League, the Smiths', Armorers' & Metalworkers' Consortium, the Thatchers', Roofers' & Masons' Circle, and many other guilds and trade associations. Some keep full-time offices, and others send a resident factor or visiting agent. The Adventurers' Guild keeps a modest agency here, with a single office, a regional notice board, and secure message boxes for members traveling the western coast. Requests from the Crown, the Preceptory, guilds, merchants, and private citizens are often posted here, and guild members leave reports of road conditions, unusual sightings, and discoveries for those who follow.</p>

<p>It is far more than a marketplace. Cargoes are arranged here, harvests negotiated, apprentices indentured, contracts signed, and disputes quietly settled, and news from across western Algar often reaches the Exchange before it reaches the village taverns.</p>

</details>

## Farms, Mills, and Forges

<details id="johunn-and-johunn-milling">
<summary><strong>Johunn & Johunn Milling</strong> — the windmill and cider press on the bay lane</summary>

<p>Johunn & Johunn Milling stands along the lane from the crossroads to the shore of Nereth Bay and is one of the region's principal agricultural businesses. The goliath brothers Harek and Borun Johunn own it and mill much of the grain harvested in this corner of the Plains of Bounty. In autumn a large fruit press also turns out juice and cider for the district's orchards. The tall windmill is a familiar landmark from the royal road and from the Preceptory's training grounds, and warehouses for grain, flour, and cider apples surround it, along with the brothers' home and yards where wagons gather at harvest.</p>

<p>The Johunns are hardworking, straightforward businessmen whose size is matched by their generosity. They keep close ties to both the village and the Preceptory, supplying flour, meal, and pressed fruit to Pistil Point, the nearby inns, and the hundreds who live and train in the sanctuary. During the busiest weeks of harvest the mill is one of the most active places outside the walls, with wagons arriving from dawn to dusk under the turning sails.</p>

</details>

<details id="queensland-orchards">
<summary><strong>Queensland Orchards</strong> — the great fruit groves along the coastal road</summary>

<p>The Queensland Orchards stretch for nearly three-quarters of a mile along the coastal road east of Pistil Point, one of the largest concentrations of fruit trees in the Duchy. The Queensland Farmers' Union owns the property and divides it into leased plots, each managed by a farming family, many of whom have tended the same groves for generations. Under the leases the growers keep most of their harvest and give the Union an agreed share, which is sorted, pressed, preserved, or packed and sent by wagon to the markets of Annapore and Knightsbridge, where Queensland apples, pears, cherries, and ciders have a fine reputation.</p>

<p>The orchards are busiest from late summer through autumn, when seasonal laborers arrive and wagons run constantly between the groves, the nearby mill, and the coast. In spring the flowering rows perfume the countryside with a sweet fragrance carried on the sea breeze, and visitors come to see them in blossom, which has made the orchards one of the defining sights of Pistil Point.</p>

</details>

<details id="hrumm-ironworks">
<summary><strong>Hrumm Ironworks</strong> — the peninsula's largest smithy</summary>

<p>Hrumm Ironworks stands just beyond the North Gate and is the peninsula's largest smithy and one of its busiest businesses. The goliath blacksmith Moder Hrumm owns and runs it. The sprawling complex has an open-air forge for large work, an enclosed forge for finer craftsmanship, workshops, warehouses, a comfortable residence, and a well-appointed showroom of weapons, armor, tools, and hardware. Moder directs four skilled smiths and apprentices, with casual laborers brought in for big commissions and busy seasons.</p>

<p>The works serve local farmers, fishermen, merchants, and the Crown, but much of its reputation rests on a long relationship with the Grand Preceptory, which it supplies with arms, armor, horseshoes, training weapons, wagon fittings, and countless other necessities, giving the smithy a quality and selection rare in a village this size. The Hrumms descend from one of several goliath families whose ship was lost off the Zephyrys coast generations ago. Sailors rescued them and brought them to Pistil Point to recover, and while many later moved on, others settled among the people who had been so generous. Their descendants, including the Hrumms and the Johunn brothers, are respected members of the community.</p>

</details>

## Harbor and Shore

<details id="public-docks">
<summary><strong>Public Docks</strong> — the waterfront for visiting vessels</summary>

<p>The Public Docks lie along the western shoreline just south of the commercial fisheries and are Pistil Point's main waterfront for visiting vessels and local maritime trade. The peninsula has no deepwater harbor, so coastal traders, fishing smacks, and the occasional merchant ship anchor offshore and transfer passengers and cargo by boat. The village keeps a sturdy launch and a large longboat to ferry people, supplies, and freight between the anchored ships and the docks, while smaller craft, fishing boats, and shallow-draft traders can tie up at the wharves directly. Warehouses, net sheds, chandlers, and modest dockside workshops support the traffic.</p>

<p>It is quieter than the busy fisheries to the north but remains the peninsula's maritime gateway. Pilgrims arriving by sea often first set foot here on their way to the Grand Preceptory, and merchants with valuable or perishable cargo often prefer the coast route to the long road. In rough weather the piers can stand nearly empty, since experienced mariners avoid a landing on the exposed coast, and a local saying holds that if the launch won't go, neither should you.</p>

</details>

<details id="myonis-beach">
<summary><strong>Myonis Beach</strong> — the village's favorite stretch of bay shore</summary>

<p>Myonis Beach runs along the sheltered shore of Nereth Bay and is the peninsula's favorite place for swimming, picnicking, beachcombing, and slow afternoons by the sea. The calm water on the lee side of Pistil Point is a welcome change from the restless Sea of Zephyrys on the western shore, and it draws families, holidaymakers, and pilgrims in the warm months. A modest public pier serves pleasure craft, small sailboats, and the occasional visiting skiff, and farther north a handful of private docks serve local crabbers and shrimpers.</p>

<p>Nereth Bay was once famous across western Algar for its fisheries and shellfish beds, and the catastrophe that struck Nereth wrecked its marine life. In recent decades the southern bay has shown encouraging signs of recovery, with shrimp and prawns plentiful, crab numbers growing, and seabirds returning to the shore, and there is hope it may one day regain some of its old abundance. Today the beach is prized more for its calm than its harvest. Children hunt for shells and polished sea glass along the tide line, anglers cast from the pier in the evening, and visitors linger to watch Lustre rise over the bay and bathe the pale sand in silver.</p>

</details>

<details id="pistil-point-fisheries">
<summary><strong>Pistil Point Fisheries</strong> — the two rival fisheries on the working docks</summary>

<p>Two privately owned fisheries stand side by side along the working docks under the oversight of the Fishermen's Compact, processing the daily catch from the Sea of Zephyrys. Both handle many kinds of seafood, but each has its own specialty. One receives, grades, packs, and dispatches fresh fish for fast transport to nearby markets, and the other brines, smokes, and cures fish into preserved seafood for communities throughout western Algar. Both keep drying racks, smokehouses, cold storage cellars, net lofts, and cooperage yards, and both buy directly from independent crews.</p>

<p>Most boats have a standing contract with one fishery or the other, though unusual catches and large hauls often go to the highest bidder. The friendly rivalry between the two has long kept prices competitive and standards high, to the benefit of the local fleet and visiting merchants. Together they are the economic backbone of the village's maritime industry, and from before dawn until well after sunset the docks fill with crews unloading, coopers mending barrels, laborers hauling crates, and merchants arranging shipments by road and sea.</p>

</details>

<details id="point-lighthouse">
<summary><strong>Point Lighthouse</strong> — the beacon at the northern tip</summary>

<p>The Point Lighthouse rises eighty feet above the rocky northern tip of the peninsula and is Pistil Point's second-tallest structure, behind only the Grand Preceptory. It is built of dressed stone and topped with a glazed lantern chamber, and it warns mariners off the peninsula's shoals, shifting sandbars, and the wide shallows that reach far into the Sea of Zephyrys. Its beacon, magnified through finely made glass lenses, burns from dusk to dawn and through fog and storm, and is visible for many miles at sea and across the prairies inland.</p>

<p>The gullfolk Toolah Tattoppo keeps the light, a cheerful woman whose keen eyes and careful habits have made her one of the village's most familiar faces. Her nightly vigils leave her days free, and she spends many of them in the village, rarely far from friendly conversation or the latest news. Few know the coast better or read the sea's moods as well. The lighthouse is also an unofficial watchtower for the northern coast, and passing vessels, distant storms, unusual lights on the sea, and drifting wreckage are noted and reported to the Crown when necessary. In bad weather villagers often glance north for the comfort of the steady light that has guided generations around the treacherous shallows.</p>

</details>

<details id="beacon-spit">
<summary><strong>Beacon Spit</strong> — the tidal islets and rope bridges beyond the lighthouse</summary>

<p>Beacon Spit reaches roughly five hundred feet into the Sea of Zephyrys beyond the Point Lighthouse, a chain of low gravel and sand islets divided by narrow tidal channels, with coarse shore grass on the highest ground. The shoals shift constantly under the wind, the waves, and the competing currents of sea and bay. Two sturdy rope bridges join the highest islets and give reliable passage at every state of the tide. At low water the channels can be waded, though few locals advise it, since the meeting tides make surprisingly strong eddies and hidden channels that can sweep an unwary visitor off their feet, and the children of Pistil Point learn early to respect them.</p>

<p>Despite the hazards, the spit is one of the peninsula's favorite gathering places. Anglers line the bridges and shore for mullet, others jig for eels, prawns, and crabs, and families spend warm afternoons among the tide pools or watching seabirds with the lighthouse behind them. Experienced local boatmen know the channels well enough to take small craft through, but larger fishing vessels and visiting ships give the spit a wide berth.</p>

<p>Each year, on the first calm evening after Highstar arrives, villagers gather on the spit to watch Lustre rise over Nereth Bay as the setting sun sinks into the Sea of Zephyrys, and for a few moments the peninsula is bathed in silver from the east and gold from the west. As twilight deepens a great bonfire is lit on the outermost islet, and families share food, music, and stories under the rising moon. There is no formal ceremony or ancient rite, only a tradition as enduring as the tides, welcoming the warmth of Highstar and celebrating the sea and the season.</p>

</details>

## Roads

<details id="shore-road-north">
<summary><strong>Shore Road North</strong> — the road along Nereth Bay toward the Dry Fells</summary>

<p>This paved royal road leaves Pistil Point to the east and bends northeast around the shores of Nereth Bay. The Crown maintains and patrols it for about twenty miles, serving the farms, orchards, and homesteads of the peninsula, and it is marked by milestones and the occasional roadside shrine to Saint Eccles where pilgrims and travelers may rest.</p>

<p>Past the last Crown milestone the fields give way to the wind-scoured sand and scrub of the Dry Fells. Patrols stop, maintenance ends, and the old highway slowly yields to the elements. Much of it is still visible, but broken paving, drifting sand, and years of neglect make it impassable to wagons, and only determined travelers on foot or horseback go on. Forty miles beyond the last settled lands it reaches the haunted ruins of Nereth, the shattered remains of one of Algar's greatest cities. On clear days travelers crossing the desolation can see the peak of Mount Guul rising nearly a hundred miles to the east, a lonely landmark that seems to watch over the abandoned road and the dead city.</p>

<details id="ohlmith-trail">
<summary><strong>Ohlmith Trail</strong> — the long road east to Ohlmith and the Queen's Road</summary>

<p>The Ohlmith Trail leaves Pistil Point to the south through the Queensland orchards and farmland. Its approach to the village is paved with dressed stone, but most of its length is a broad, well-kept, hard-packed road suited to wagons, caravans, and mounted patrols all year. After about forty miles it turns east across the farming heartland of northern Queensland, through scattered villages, market hamlets, and prosperous farms, staying well south of the Dry Fells and north of the Rolling Hills.</p>

<p>It runs nearly two hundred miles east to the city of Ohlmith, where it joins the Queen's Road, Algar's great north-south trade route, and so links the fisheries, orchards, and farms of Queensland to every major kingdom and freehold on the continent. The market town of Crannick, about halfway, is a popular overnight stop and the practical limit of patrols from Pistil Point, after which the Crown garrisons and reeves of the eastern districts take over. Locals rarely think of the road as ending at Ohlmith. To farmers, merchants, and pilgrims it is the road that ties Queensland to the markets, guilds, and trade routes of the interior, and at harvest long wagon trains of apples, cider, grain, and preserved fish are a familiar sight on it.</p>

</details>

<details id="shore-road-south">
<summary><strong>Shore Road South</strong> — the coastal highway to Annapore</summary>

<p>This paved royal highway follows Algar's western coast for nearly a hundred miles between Pistil Point and the port city of Annapore. It is maintained year round and jointly patrolled by the Crown, with the Pistil Point garrison responsible for the northern section and patrols from Annapore covering the rest. As it enters the village it passes directly before the gates of the Grand Preceptory of Saint Eccles, a familiar route for pilgrims, merchants, clergy, and travelers headed for Elderland's foremost Ecclesian sanctuary.</p>

<p>The countryside along it is among the most productive in western Algar, with rolling farmland, prosperous orchards, and small fishing hamlets, and its bridges, milestones, and roadside shrines to Saint Eccles reflect centuries of royal investment and faithful stewardship. Wagons of cider, grain, timber, and salted fish are common, as are pilgrims on foot under the saint's lantern. For most visitors it gives the first view of Pistil Point, where the halls of the Preceptory rise above the orchards long before the village walls appear.</p>

</details>
