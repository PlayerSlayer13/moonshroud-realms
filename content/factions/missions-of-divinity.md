# Missions of Divinity

The Missions of Divinity — often called simply Divine Missions — are the rarest and most
consequential path to sanctioned work in Algar. Where the Adventurer's Guild rewards service
and the Royal Adventuring Society rewards service to the Crown, Divine Missions are granted
only when a matter is judged to carry spiritual, cosmic, or multi-faith significance. Most
parties won't encounter this path until their reputation and capability have already grown
considerably — by the time divine attention turns toward them, their actions have usually
already begun intersecting with forces beyond the mundane.

## Halinaar and the Seat of Divine Authority

All divine missions are sanctioned in Halinaar City, the religious center of Elderland and the
sole authority for divine adjudication. The dominion of Halinaar exists as neutral sacred
ground, bordered by rival powers yet beholden to none. Its capital stands on an island in the
Lake of Truth, a place of pilgrimage, judgment, and revelation, where temples of the Eternals
converge and every divine mission is reviewed, refined, and issued. Where other nations
project power through armies or trade, Halinaar exerts influence through belief, legitimacy,
and access to divine favor.

Because every mission passes through this single central body, divine work is inherently
limited in opportunity and deliberate in scope — nothing here is issued casually.

## How Missions Arrive

The Missions of Divinity keep no field agents. Instead, they rely on established religious
hierarchies to receive and distribute sanctioned work, delivered through one of three methods:

- **Divine Conveyance** — In matters of real urgency, a high-ranking divine practitioner transmits the mission directly to a distant temple authority: not mass teleportation, but a focused act of will delivering a sealed writ, inscribed object, or manifested message into the care of a designated Prior or Abbot near the site of concern.
- **Revelation** — A receiving cleric or temple authority is granted the mission through ritual, vision, or sacred inscription — script formed during prayer, or knowledge impressed upon the mind and later transcribed.
- **Messenger** — A formal parchment bearing Halinaar's authority, delivered physically by a trusted courier or pilgrim.

In every case, missions are never publicly posted. They are entrusted.

## Mission Requirements

Access to a divine mission isn't purchased, granted by status, or negotiated through
influence. It's recognized. Unlike the Guild or the Society, the Missions of Divinity require
no fee, no title, and no formal registration — but they aren't open to just anyone. A party
seeking to receive and complete a sanctioned divine mission needs at least one member capable
of serving as a genuine spiritual intermediary: someone with real standing, training, or
personal connection to the divine, willing to act as the point of contact between the group
and the divine order. That person doesn't need to lead the party, but they need to be present
and willing to receive visions, interpret sacred writs, or be formally entrusted with the
mission by temple authorities.

Without such a figure, a party may still encounter divine events, or even act in ways that
align with the will of the Eternals — but they can't formally receive or complete a sanctioned
divine mission, nor be recognized for it afterward.

### The Divine Intermediary

That role doesn't have to belong to a player character. If a party lacks anyone who can
qualify, a temple may assign someone to accompany them instead — a figure sent to observe and
report, tasked with safeguarding a sacred site, bound to ensure the mission is carried out
faithfully, or simply an acolyte who receives visions they barely understand themselves. Their
purpose isn't to lead. It's to anchor the mission in the divine. They carry authority in
matters of faith, but rely on the party for survival, problem-solving, and getting the work
done.

## Divine Chits and Boons

Completing a divine mission earns divine chits — a third form of recognition alongside guild
and noble chits, but one that carries a distinct weight. Where guild chits represent skill and
reputation, and noble chits represent influence and service to power, divine chits represent
recognized alignment with divine purpose. They aren't simply earned through service rendered
or tasks completed under contract; they reflect something the divine order itself has chosen
to acknowledge.

Divine rewards typically take the form of blessings and boons, access to sacred or restricted
knowledge, and rare relics tied to the Eternals — the specifics of which are left to the
temple or authority granting them. The more significant a boon, the more likely it is to
require travel to Halinaar City itself to receive it; lesser blessings can be conferred at
major temples with sufficiently high-ranking clergy present to perform the rite.

## What People Know

Most residents of Algar have never met anyone who's received a divine mission, and wouldn't
recognize the signs if they had. The Missions of Divinity operate quietly, through channels
few outside the temples themselves fully understand — which is, by design, exactly how Halinaar
prefers it.
