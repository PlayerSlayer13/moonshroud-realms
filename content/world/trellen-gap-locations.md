# Trellen Gap Locations

Notable sites within the hamlet of Trellen Gap, presented roughly in the order a traveler
would encounter them moving from the roads into the settlement proper and out again into the
forest beyond.

<details id="riverside-road">
<summary><strong>Riverside Road</strong> — the hamlet's southern trade artery</summary>

<p>A hard-packed wagon road forming Trellen Gap's primary southern route, Riverside Road runs
for just over a hundred miles through the Great Forest toward the town of Riverside, following
the Chasm River for much of its length. Beyond Riverside it continues to Gibbons Station on
the Cholm River at the border with Felgar, joining the broader Cholm Trade Corridor that links
southern Algar to the ducal seat at Rhundel.</p>

<p>The road isn't regularly patrolled, but Crown inspectors and work crews visit periodically
to keep it passable, and military escorts occasionally accompany dignitaries, tax officials, or
caravans of importance.</p>

</details>

<details id="watch-huts">
<summary><strong>Watch Huts</strong> — seven early-warning posts ringing the palisade</summary>

<p>Scattered beyond each opening in the palisade — from a few paces to as far as three hundred
feet into the forest — stand seven small watch huts, each manned by a single volunteer on a
rotating schedule. These watchers aren't true guards but early-warning sentinels, reporting
anything unusual and signaling with a horn: a pair of short blasts for approaching riders or a
group on the road, longer urgent calls for danger.</p>

<p>Each hut offers basic shelter — a simple roof and a small stove for colder weather — and
though built for one, can hold four or five people in an emergency.</p>

</details>

<details id="south-gate">
<summary><strong>South Gate</strong> — the hamlet's southern entrance</summary>

<p>Where Riverside Road passes through the palisade, the South Gate consists of a wagon-wide
opening with no permanent doors — just a stack of heavy hardwood logs nearby, ready to be
rolled into place as a barricade at night or in times of danger. A small guard's hut stands to
the east, staffed by a militia volunteer from dawn until dusk.</p>

<p>Visitors are met and questioned before being allowed through. The watch isn't overly strict,
but it's attentive, and unfamiliar or evasive travelers are remembered and quietly reported
within the hamlet.</p>

</details>

<details id="shrine-of-the-luminous-birch">
<summary><strong>Shrine of the Luminous Birch</strong> — Trellen Gap's sole place of worship</summary>

<p>Set along the hamlet's eastern edge, the Shrine of the Luminous Birch is one of Trellen
Gap's oldest surviving structures, built of pale stone reclaimed from the nearby aelfaen
ruins. Its bell tower rises above the rooftops, rung not only for rites but for gatherings,
warnings, and moments of communal importance. Inside, light filters through simple glass panes
onto a carved representation of the Luminous Birch etched into a stone altar.</p>

<p>The shrine is devoted to <a href="{{< relref "the-gods#luaur" >}}">Luaur</a>, and tended by
Deldar Navalur, a young deacon whose duties extend well past formal devotion — counseling
laborers, mediating small disputes, and offering comfort in hardship, much as any keeper of a
community this isolated must. Woodcutters and travelers often pause to leave small offerings
before setting into the forest, and returning hunters give quiet thanks beneath the birch's
symbol.</p>

</details>

<details id="great-forest-sundry">
<summary><strong>Great Forest Sundry</strong> — the hamlet's outfitter and general supply</summary>

<p>A sturdy two-story timber building with iron-braced walls and a wide overhang for browsing
customers, Great Forest Sundry is Trellen Gap's primary outfitter — rope, lantern oil,
torches, rations, bedrolls, tools, and replacement gear for travelers passing through the
forest. A rack near the counter holds bundled arrows and a few serviceable weapons suited for
self-defense rather than war.</p>

<p>The shop is owned by Marra Noakes, a sharp-eyed halfling affiliated with the Merchants'
Exchange who also buys pelts, preserved game, herbs, and other goods brought in from the
forest — at her discretion, and often at a reduced rate for anything of unclear origin. The
shop doubles as a quiet hub of information, where travelers trade news and Marra keeps half an
ear on the current state of the forest.</p>

</details>

<details id="greenhook-exchange">
<summary><strong>Greenhook Exchange</strong> — the hamlet's central hub for trade and contracts</summary>

<p>A sturdy two-story timber building standing nearly opposite the Sundry, the Greenhook
Exchange handles trade, contracts, and provisions for Trellen Gap. The ground floor is given
over to stacked crates, sealed barrels, and a broad counter where agreements are drafted; the
proprietor's living quarters occupy the floor above.</p>

<p>The Exchange is run by Dorian Voss, a measured human trader affiliated with the Merchants'
Exchange who deals in quantities rather than curios — flour, grains, preserved foods, and
other staples sourced from passing merchants and routed through Riverside. He also purchases
forest goods in bulk, and coordinates the transport arrangements that keep supply moving
through the Gap. An adjacent lot hosts a regular market day, when locals and forest dwellers
sell seasonal produce and handmade goods.</p>

</details>

<details id="clumsy-apple-inn">
<summary><strong>Clumsy Apple Inn</strong> — the hamlet's principal lodging and gathering place</summary>

<p>A sprawling, L-shaped three-story timber structure wrapped around a packed yard, the Clumsy
Apple takes its name from a weathered sign depicting a cartoonish apple figure mid-stumble,
ale sloshing from its mug. A large stable to the west houses several teams of horses, with a
hayloft above for tack, feed, and overflow guests when the inn is full.</p>

<p>The inn is run by Tamsin Brindlebarrel, a cheerful, no-nonsense halfling who keeps a tight
operation and a keen sense for trouble before it fully arrives at her door. The ground-floor
common room, built around a well-used hearth, is the social center of Trellen Gap — news
travels quickly here, deals get struck over ale, and the mood of the forest gets measured in
quiet conversation.</p>

</details>

<details id="reeves-manor">
<summary><strong>Reeve's Manor</strong> — the administrative heart of Trellen Gap</summary>

<p>A well-kept timber residence along the main street, maintained to a higher standard than
most homes in the hamlet without tipping into grandeur, Reeve's Manor is where Trellen Gap's
governance actually happens — a modest front room doubling as an office, records kept in
simple ledgers, visitors received at a sturdy table rather than behind a formal desk.</p>

<p>The current reeve is Elira Vanneth, an even-tempered aelfaen appointed by the Duchy of
Rhundel for her steady judgment. She handles trade disagreements, boundary concerns, and
reports of unusual activity in the forest, working closely with the militia captains since
there's no standing guard to call on. In an emergency, the shrine's bell or the watch huts'
horns serve as the call to gather.</p>

</details>

<details id="phoenix-forge">
<summary><strong>Phoenix Forge</strong> — the hamlet's blacksmith</summary>

<p>One of the few stone-built structures in Trellen Gap, Phoenix Forge stands just west of the
Clumsy Apple's stable — a broad, blocky workshop with thick walls and a soot-darkened chimney,
a small wooden dwelling built directly onto its roof. Inside, a large hearth dominates one
side of the space, anvils and tool racks arranged with efficient practicality.</p>

<p>The forge is owned and worked by Gar Red-Phoenix, an ironbark ursinor affiliated with the
Smiths', Armorers' & Metalworkers' Consortium. He produces and repairs the tools that keep
Trellen Gap functioning — axes, saws, nails, wagon fittings, palisade ironwork — along with
weapons and armor when the need arises. Without the forge, tools would dull and defenses would
weaken; it's less a business than a pillar of the hamlet's endurance.</p>

</details>

<details id="muster-green">
<summary><strong>Muster Green</strong> — the hamlet's civic gathering space</summary>

<p>An open, park-like space near the heart of Trellen Gap's south wall, Muster Green centers
on a packed-earth wagon turnaround bordered by pruned shrubs and shade trees, with a
stone-lined well at its middle. A weathered statue of one of the hamlet's founders — a locally
celebrated adventurer — stands at the southern edge.</p>

<p>A sturdy muster hall along the green serves as the militia's organizational hub, where those
assigned to watch or patrol gather each pre-dawn for their duties, and doubles as a modest
armory holding training bows, bowstrings, arrows, and spare shortswords. On quiet days it's
simply a place to draw water and exchange news; in times of alarm, it becomes the settlement's
point of coordination.</p>

</details>

<details id="fields-and-farming-cooperative">
<summary><strong>Fields and Farming Cooperative</strong> — the hamlet's agricultural base</summary>

<p>The southern third of Trellen Gap is given over to fields, barns, and outbuildings, worked
collectively by a local cooperative of six to ten households depending on the season, with
labor, tools, and yields shared and rotated as needed. The cooperative maintains a loose
affiliation with the Growers' and Harvesters' Guild, though contact is infrequent given the
hamlet's isolation.</p>

<p>Three plots are cultivated: western fields for potatoes and oats, an eastern field for
mixed vegetables, and modest livestock kept by individual families rather than the cooperative
as a whole. Harvest periods turn this into one of the busiest corners of the hamlet; outside
of them, the fields stay quiet but carefully tended.</p>

</details>

<details id="redgrove-millworks">
<summary><strong>Redgrove Millworks</strong> — the hamlet's timber operation</summary>

<p>Running along much of Trellen Gap's northern palisade, Redgrove Millworks is the hamlet's
largest private enterprise, centered on a tall wind-driven sawmill audible across the
settlement when the wind is right. Two large drying sheds and several smaller outbuildings
surround it, with a dedicated service road letting wagons move freely between the mill and the
gates without clogging the main street.</p>

<p>The mill is owned by Harlan Redgrove, a broad-shouldered human affiliated with the
Carpenters' & Shipwrights' Guild, who coordinates a network of local woodcutters, hunters, and
forest guides bringing in timber from designated cutting areas. Most of the output supports
local needs — reinforcing buildings, repairing defenses — though a few wagon shipments leave
the hamlet for export when conditions allow.</p>

</details>

<details id="path-gates">
<summary><strong>Path Gates</strong> — four informal footpath entrances</summary>

<p>Beyond its three main wagon entrances, Trellen Gap maintains four smaller openings in the
palisade connecting directly to forest footpaths — narrower and less formal, meant for daily
movement rather than trade traffic. Villagers use them for hunting, fishing, foraging, and
woodcutting, or as quick access back to safety when working beyond the walls.</p>

<p>Each opening is wide enough for one or two people at a time, barricaded from dusk until
dawn and typically left open and unguarded during daylight, though each is indirectly watched
by a nearby watch hut. The four paths lead, respectively: southwest past a druid's hut deeper
in the forest, south near Muster Green climbing toward the ridge, east into the shrine
grounds' ironwork boundary, and northeast down toward the Aelthrane River.</p>

</details>

<details id="vaelis-thornebloom-s-hut">
<summary><strong>Vaelis Thornebloom's Hut</strong> — home of a druid of the Circle of the Seven Moons</summary>

<p>Just beyond the southwestern path gate, across a narrow stream called Ribbon Creek, stands
a low, moss-roofed hut among leaning hardwoods — reached by a footbridge of a single fallen
trunk, often damp with mist even in fair weather. A small, carefully tended clearing surrounds
it, marked with deliberate arrangements of stones, shallow bowls of water, and pale wood
markers.</p>

<p>The hut belongs to Vaelis Thornebloom, an aelfaen druid of the Circle of the Seven Moons.
Known and accepted in Trellen Gap without being fully part of it, she's sought out for natural
remedies, guidance on forest paths, and matters that fall outside common understanding — asking
little in return beyond occasional supplies or news of the forest. Her manner is calm and
distant, marked by long pauses and an unsettling habit of watching the sky even in daylight,
though she's regarded with quiet respect rather than fear.</p>

</details>

<details id="west-gate">
<summary><strong>West Gate</strong> — the hamlet's connection to the forest interior</summary>

<p>The West Gate marks Trellen Gap's primary link to the interior of the Great Forest, where
the Great Forest Trail meets the settlement before continuing east. Less traveled than
Riverside Road, it still sees regular use from hunters, woodcutters, and those bound toward
Rhundel by the most direct route available.</p>

<p>Like the other gates, it's a wagon-wide opening with barricade logs kept nearby, watched
during daylight by a militia volunteer from a small guard's hut. Traffic here tends to be
quieter and more familiar than at the South Gate, though strangers from the forest are still
met with measured scrutiny.</p>

</details>

<details id="great-forest-trail">
<summary><strong>Great Forest Trail</strong> — the Crown road toward Rhundel</summary>

<p>Departing through the West Gate, the Great Forest Trail threads westward through the deep
woods as the most direct route to the ducal seat at Rhundel, used by officials, patrols, and
seasoned travelers. After crossing the Chasm River it splits — the main route bearing
southwest toward the forest-edge town of Sunshade before continuing to Rhundel, and a lesser
branch, the Lake Road, continuing to Foam Lake and beyond it toward Fort Garian.</p>

<p>Crown crews keep the trail passable, and duchy patrols pass through several times a year,
making camp just beyond the hamlet's walls and bringing a noticeable surge of coin and
activity to Trellen Gap's businesses during their stay.</p>

</details>

<details id="north-gate">
<summary><strong>North Gate</strong> — terminus of the Claydon Trail</summary>

<p>Set into the northwest corner of the palisade, the North Gate marks the start of the
Claydon Trail, a rugged road climbing into the Camiain foothills. It's the hamlet's most
active gate by daily traffic — wagons laden with raw timber pass through constantly, bound for
nearby Redgrove Millworks — watched during daylight from a small guard hut on the southwest
side of the opening.</p>

</details>

<details id="claydon-trail">
<summary><strong>Claydon Trail</strong> — the rugged northern road into the Camiain Hills</summary>

<p>Departing through the North Gate, the Claydon Trail winds north beneath the forest canopy
before climbing through the Claydon Pass into the Camiain Hills. It receives far less
maintenance than the Great Forest Trail, and ruts, roots, and soft earth are common
underfoot — though its southern stretches see steady use from timber crews, trappers, and
charcoal burners feeding the hamlet's mills.</p>

<p>Roughly ten miles north, the terrain shifts and traffic grows sparse. Lesser paths branch
off along the way — one east toward the enigmatic Attan Obelisk, one west toward the frontier
town of Jewel Prairie — while the main trail continues nearly a hundred miles to its terminus
at Hillview, beneath the Monolith at the base of the Livingstone Mountains. Beyond its
southern reaches the road is unpredictable enough that travelers are advised to move in
company and stay alert.</p>

</details>

<details id="whitwood-timber-camp">
<summary><strong>Whitwood Timber Camp</strong> — the closest of several timber camps to Trellen Gap</summary>

<p>Nestled along Cord Creek, this practical rather than permanent camp — a central firepit,
canvas tents among the trees, stacked and sorted logs awaiting transport — serves as the
operational heart of Duenna Whitwood's timber holdings, and the one most frequently visited by
locals and passing adventurers.</p>

<p>Duenna, a sharp-eyed halfling, owns several small woodlots spread across the forest, worked
in rotation and deliberately limited in size to prevent overharvesting. She's rarely idle,
walking between her claims most days to inspect work and mark future cuts, sometimes covering
twenty miles in a single day despite her stature. She pays well for reliable help and keeps a
close ear to any reports of unusual tracks or disturbances in the deeper woods.</p>

</details>

<details id="aelthrane-ruins">
<summary><strong>Aelthrane Ruins</strong> — the remains of an ancient aelfaen settlement</summary>

<p>Scattered along the banks of the Aelthrane River northeast of Trellen Gap, spilling over
Cord Creek across nearly two hundred thousand square feet, these ruins are what's left of a
settlement long surrendered to the forest — moss-covered arches, fractured foundations, and
the circular mouths of old wells softened by age but not entirely reclaimed.</p>

<p>Children play among the outer stones in daylight, tolerated only at the fringes; the
hamlet's elders speak firmly against deeper exploration, their warnings repeated often enough
to carry real weight. Some stones resist collapse, some paths seem to re-emerge no matter how
often they're lost, and one clearing at the ruins' heart — a raised, level grassy circle,
untouched by the forest's slow creep — is said to mark a dormant crossing between the Material
Plane and the fey realms, once watched over by an aelfaen warden bastion. Those who linger
there too long sometimes report a faint sense of displacement, as though the world has shifted
just slightly out of alignment.</p>

</details>
