# Talorin

*Provisional. The Talorin framework comes from correspondence with Sandy Lawson and has not
yet appeared in a Soulstone issue. Details may change when it is published.*

Talorin are avian humanoids native to the mountainous territory north of Algar, with
additional holdings scattered along the Cholm Trade Corridor. Every Talorin descends from
one of four raptor lineages, and each lineage keeps the flight, keen senses, and natural
talons of the bird it traces its ancestry to.

---

## In the World

### Origins and Culture

A full Talorin society exists in the mountains north of Algar, though it remains largely
undocumented on Orb at large.

### The Four Lineages

Each lineage takes its name from the raptor it descends from: Duskwing (owl), Stormwing
(eagle), Swiftwing (hawk), and Gravewing (vulture). All four share the Talon Flight feature
and differ in their second feature.

---

## Playing a Talorin

*The following is for players creating a Talorin character using the Daggerheart system.
Talorin are treated as four separate ancestries by lineage. Mixed ancestry characters take
the primary feature of their main ancestry and the secondary feature of their other
ancestry.*

<details>
<summary><strong>Duskwing</strong></summary>

<p>Duskwing talorin (Noctae, owlfolk) are built for silence and low light: mottled,
soft-feathered, with wide eyes suited to dusk and dark. They favor night work such as
watch-keeping, message-running after curfew, and trades that prize quiet over speed.</p>

<p><strong>Talon Flight</strong><br>
You have wings and can fly. Your unarmed strikes deal 1d4 physical damage using your
Proficiency.</p>

<p><strong>Night Hunter</strong><br>
You can see clearly in darkness up to Close range, and you have Advantage on Instinct Rolls
made to move or act without being detected while in darkness or dim light.</p>

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Stormwing</strong></summary>

<p>Stormwing talorin (Aquilae, eaglefolk) are broad-winged and heavy-boned for their kind,
built for altitude and impact rather than stealth. Many gravitate toward soldiering,
falconry-adjacent trades, or anything that rewards being the largest thing in the sky.</p>

<p><strong>Talon Flight</strong><br>
You have wings and can fly. Your unarmed strikes deal 1d4 physical damage using your
Proficiency.</p>

<p><strong>Aerial Strike</strong><br>
Once per scene, you may dive from height to make an attack. If you do, add a d6 to your
damage roll and move up to Far range as part of the attack.</p>

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Swiftwing</strong></summary>

<p>Swiftwing talorin (Astorae, hawkfolk) are compact and quick, built for pursuit rather
than power. They take naturally to courier work, scouting, and anything where getting
there first matters more than arriving heavy.</p>

<p><strong>Talon Flight</strong><br>
You have wings and can fly. Your unarmed strikes deal 1d4 physical damage using your
Proficiency.</p>

<p><strong>Swift Flight</strong><br>
While flying, you ignore difficult terrain such as turbulent air, cluttered airspace, and
similar impediments (not solid obstructions like walls or enclosed rooms). Once per rest,
you may spend a Hope to fly to any point you can see within Far range as part of your
movement.</p>

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Gravewing</strong></summary>

<p>Gravewing talorin (Vulturae, vulturefolk) are broad, bald-headed, and unbothered by
things that trouble everyone else: spoiled food, disease, the presence of the dead. They
are often found where others won't go, in battlefield cleanup, plague wards, and the parts
of a city that keep quiet business.</p>

<p><strong>Talon Flight</strong><br>
You have wings and can fly. Your unarmed strikes deal 1d4 physical damage using your
Proficiency.</p>

<p><strong>Carrion Feeder</strong><br>
You suffer no ill effects from spoiled food, tainted drink, or the presence of disease and
decay, and you have Advantage on rolls to endure them.</p>

</details>
