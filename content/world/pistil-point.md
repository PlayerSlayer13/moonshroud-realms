# Pistil Point

Pistil Point is a modest walled village on a narrow peninsula where the sheltered waters of Nereth Bay meet the open Sea of Zephyrys. About two hundred people live inside the walls, and several hundred more live on the farms, orchards, and homesteads around it. It is neither a great port nor a center of commerce. Its reputation rests on two callings, the fishing fleets that have fed the village for generations and the towering Grand Preceptory of Saint Eccles, which rises beyond the southern gate.

## The Preceptory

The sandstone sanctuary is more than six hundred feet long and can be seen far out on the Sea of Zephyrys and across the prairies to the east. Pilgrims come throughout the year to worship, and squires, priests, and aspiring paladins travel from across the kingdom to train within its halls. Its clerics, paladins, and temple wardens have watched over the region for centuries, and few would willingly threaten a village that lies under the gaze of one of Elderland's best-known religious orders.

## The Crown and the Reeve

Pistil Point lies within the Royal Burg of Queensland, the Crown district around the capital at Knightsbridge. A permanent detachment of Crown soldiers patrols the royal roads from the village, protecting merchants, pilgrims, and the isolated farms of the western Plains of Bounty. The Royal Reeve, of House Bastelin, represents the Crown in the village and the district around it.

## Life on the Point

Fishing boats leave each dawn to cast their nets in the clean waters of the Sea of Zephyrys. The Gold River still carries traces of the Dry Fells' lingering corruption into the eastern reaches of Nereth Bay, but the waters nearest the village have slowly recovered, and the bay's calm shallows again welcome swimmers, beachgoers, and small pleasure craft. Farmers and orchard keepers bring in produce from the countryside, and pilgrims fill the village inns before continuing to the Preceptory.

## A Place People Seek Out

Since the fall of Nereth cut the old coastal road generations ago, Pistil Point has stopped being a place travelers pass through. Few visitors arrive by chance. It is a place they come to on purpose, a village where journeys end and new callings begin, and where the legacy of Saint Eccles shapes every road that leads to its gates.
