## The Crown and the Castle

<details id="cairn-castle">
<summary><strong>Cairn Castle</strong> — the old keep above the sea</summary>

<p>Cairn Castle rises over the royal compound in the northwest corner of the walls, its central keep flanked by twin turrets and looking out over both the Sea of Zephyrys and Godsfall Bay. The foundations are very old, and in places the oldest stonework still shows through the repairs and reinforcements of later ages. Behind the keep, the castle staff tend a royal garden to exacting standards, one of the few quiet places inside the fortified grounds.</p>

<p>The barony's seat has since moved fifty miles south to Sand Manor, but Baron Sand keeps chambers here and comes back to the castle when duty calls. A full suite of royal apartments is also kept ready for members of the monarchy, though such visits are rare and brief. In the meantime the castle is far from idle. A chamberlain and household servants run it, a full complement of guards holds the inner bailey, and its stables, chapel, armory, barracks, and halls could receive a lord or the Crown itself at a moment's notice.</p>

</details>

<details id="royal-compound">
<summary><strong>Royal Compound</strong> — the fortified heart of Crown authority</summary>

<p>The Royal Compound encircles Cairn Castle and forms a fortified district of its own within the town walls, the administrative and military heart of Crown authority in Cairnellah. Its outer bailey opens onto the coast road beyond the walls and onto several of the town's main streets, so soldiers, supplies, and official traffic can come and go without clogging the rest of the town. The seneschal, the Crown's appointed authority, lives within the compound and oversees daily governance. A prominent temple of <a href="{{< relref "the-gods#kelleth" >}}">Kelleth</a> anchors the grounds, a place of devotion and a reminder of duty.</p>

<p>The Crown's military presence here is separate from the guard assigned to the castle's inner bailey. Barracks for enlisted soldiers line one side, with a junior ranks hall, a mess, and an officers' club close by, while several stables house mounted patrols and messengers. An armory and quartermaster's hall keep the garrison supplied. At the center, the headquarters and the commander's residence coordinate patrols, coastal watch, and inland security. For all its walls, the compound is never cut off from the town, and it stays in constant contact with everyday life in Cairnellah.</p>

</details>

<details id="town-office">
<summary><strong>Town Office</strong> — the administrative heart of the town</summary>

<p>The Town Office stands near the center of Cairnellah as its administrative heart. It is built of polished brown marble with brass fittings, and its broad frontage, tall windows, and reinforced doors suggest permanence and authority without the open militarism of the royal compound. The seneschal's chambers are here, where royal interests are overseen in the absence of direct noble authority. Decrees are issued from the building, disputes are reviewed, and coordination between the Crown, the guilds, and local leaders is maintained. Clerks, recordkeepers, and administrators fill the surrounding halls, keeping taxation records, shipping manifests, trade agreements, and land holdings, and messengers carry updates between the docks, the guildhalls, and the royal compound.</p>

<p>The office is open to the public in daylight hours, and residents come to file claims, settle disputes, register contracts, or seek permits for trade and construction. The process is slow but generally regarded as fair, and the clerks are known for their efficiency within the system. For adventurers it is a point of official contact, particularly when Crown authority, legal standing, or large-scale trade is involved, and a useful source of information for those who know how to ask.</p>

</details>

<details id="castle-gate">
<summary><strong>Castle Gate</strong> — the restricted entry to the castle's outer bailey</summary>

<p>The Castle Gate gives direct access to the outer bailey of Cairn Castle. Twin stone turrets form a compact barbican that controls passage through an iron portcullis and a reinforced wagon gate. Unlike the town's public gates, it is built for security and control, not traffic. It opens onto the same northern shoreline as the North Gate, but access is tightly restricted, and entry is granted only by express invitation or for scheduled military movement.</p>

<p>Crown soldiers stationed in the barbican enforce this without exception, vetting messengers, officials, and supply wagons bound for the castle and turning away anyone who approaches without leave. The public seldom uses it, but it gives the royal compound a secure link to the northern approaches and reinforces the castle's independence within the walls.</p>

</details>

## Sea and Sky

<details id="gravenshoal-skerries">
<summary><strong>Gravenshoal Skerries</strong> — the dangerous rocks and lighthouse offshore</summary>

<p>Where Godsfall Bay meets the open sea, just off the northwest edge of Cairnellah, the Gravenshoal Skerries rise out of the water like broken teeth. Their dark shapes seem to shift with the tide and spray, and more than one careless vessel has been lost on them. The largest skerry is about 225 feet long and 100 feet across at its widest, and a fortified lighthouse stands on its western edge. The beacon warns ships off the rocks, and a ballista on the tower covers the approaches to Cairnellah's walls and waters.</p>

<p>The rock is held in shared responsibility. The Crown military mans the fortifications and weaponry, while civilian guild members serve as trained keepers of the light, tending the beacon and its mechanisms. A small outbuilding near the landing stores provisions, and two narrow piers and a longboat ferry people and supplies to the mainland, though crossings depend on weather and tide and are not always easy.</p>

</details>

<details id="town-docks">
<summary><strong>Town Docks</strong> — the busy harbor front along the western wall</summary>

<p>The Town Docks run for nearly twelve hundred feet along Cairnellah's western wall, a continuous line of stonework facing the Sea of Zephyrys with eight substantial piers reaching into deeper water. The docks are sorted by purpose. The northern piers handle lighter commerce, from small coastal vessels to ferries to and from the Bimbot Lands, while the larger berths farther south take bulk cargo under the eye of dock factors and guild agents. The shipyard section lies beside them, and a small, secured dock is kept for private lease by merchants, officials, and anyone else who prefers discretion.</p>

<p>The whole front is fortified, with stone sea walls, timber works, watchtowers, and ballistae kept by Crown forces and trained crews. Warehouses and counting houses crowd the inner edge, cranes and winches move the cargo, and harbor masters assign berths and collect fees. The traffic is regulated, but it never really stops.</p>

</details>

<details id="able-and-codd-shipyards">
<summary><strong>Able & Codd Shipyards</strong> — the shipwrights of the dockside</summary>

<p>Able & Codd occupies a prominent stretch of the dockside and is one of Cairnellah's most industrious businesses. It is jointly owned by Zachary Able, a pragmatic human businessman, and Jarmon Codd, a meticulous gnome shipwright who is also the local Guildmaster of the Carpenters' & Shipwrights' Guild. The yard is best known for its sturdy coastal trading ketches, prized along Algar's western waters, and on commission it can also build two-masted schooners, though such jobs are rare. It is better known day to day as a repair hub, fixing sails, rudders, helms, and surface wear.</p>

<p>The dry dock cannot take a major hull reconstruction, but ships too battered to reach the larger yards at Bridgeport or Annopore often find temporary salvation here, where crews can stabilize the damage and prepare for the journey onward. The yard depends on a steady supply of timber, much of it from Wexford and Bridgeport, which makes it a link between the inland logging camps and the sea. It is also known for its artisanal greencap helms. The yard is a place of constant motion, loud with tools and rigging and smelling of pitch and sawdust.</p>

</details>

<details id="cairnellah-fishery">
<summary><strong>Cairnellah Fishery</strong> — the working shoreline south of the walls</summary>

<p>Just south of the town walls, where the coast opens up into working shoreline, stands Cairnellah's fishery, one of the town's largest and hardest-working industries. Every day dories and small boats return from the Sea of Zephyrys with cod, salmon, and other coastal catch. Along the shore, long wooden tables and stone troughs form the lines where fish are gutted, cleaned, and scaled, and the fresh catch is hurried through the nearby gates to the town markets and its fishmongers, inns, and passing merchants.</p>

<p>Not everything is sold fresh. Drying racks line the outer grounds for salted fish, coopers pack barrels of preserved stock, smokehouses turn out smoked cuts, and some of the catch is pickled in brine for export to inland settlements and coastal partners. Counting the crews at sea along with the processors, packers, and carters, the fishery is one of Cairnellah's biggest sources of work, and it feeds the town's trade network across Algar and beyond. The air smells of salt and labor, and the work rarely stops.</p>

</details>

<details id="godsfall-artifact">
<summary><strong>Godsfall Artifact</strong> — the dragon palace beneath the airship anchorage</summary>

<p>The Godsfall Artifact is what remains of a dragon palace that once dominated the region, buried beneath the modern town. The ruins predate both aelfaen and humans. A great reception hall and landing area have been restored, enough to hint at the scale of the power once centered here. It was first a park and a monument to antiquity, and later, through a treaty with the gnomes of the Bimbot Lands, it became an airship anchorage.</p>

<p>The immense dome can still host dragons, though they are rare, and serves as a grand terminal for gnome-built vessels. Its ancient runes are said to support powerful workings, but anything of that kind requires royal assent. The airship landing field inside the walls is a large part of why gnomish travelers and merchants favor Cairnellah.</p>

</details>

## Guildhalls

<details id="carpenters-and-shipwrights-guildhall">
<summary><strong>Carpenters' & Shipwrights' Guildhall</strong> — the guild of woodcraft and shipbuilding</summary>

<p>Close to the shipyards on the western edge of town, the Carpenters' & Shipwrights' Guildhall is built the way its members work, of heavy timber with reinforced stone supports. From here the guild oversees construction standards, hull repair, and the safe use of scaffolding and rigging throughout Cairnellah. It also governs timber rights and supply, deciding how wood from inland sources is divided between shipbuilding, repairs, and local construction, which puts the guild at the meeting point of forest and harbor.</p>

<p>The guildmaster presides, backed by senior shipwrights and master carpenters who inspect work, settle disputes, and approve major projects. Their authority extends over the shipyards and into any town structure where safety is at stake, which gives the guild a respected voice in civic and commercial matters alike. Beside the hall stands a well-used feasthall, a large open room where crews and craftsmen share meals, strike deals, and celebrate finished jobs, and it is rarely quiet.</p>

</details>

<details id="adventurers-guild">
<summary><strong>Adventurers' Guild, Cairnellah</strong> — the local hall for sanctioned work</summary>

<p>The Adventurers' Guild occupies a walled lot in the southwest quarter of town, not far from the docks and the nearby inns. It is smaller than the halls in the larger cities but serves traveling parties and resident members well. It has a compact training yard where recruits and veterans test their weapons and spells, and adjacent facilities for certification trials overseen by guild officials. A guild guard captain and assigned personnel keep the grounds secure and the testing safe.</p>

<p>A guildmaster runs the chapter and keeps in contact with other branches. A membership administrator handles registrations, dues, and records, a mission allocator assigns work according to certification, and an examiner of arms assesses combat readiness and conducts formal trials. Postings in the main hall range from local concerns, such as escort work, coastal disturbances, and missing persons, to distant contracts tied to trade routes, inland settlements, and sea ventures. As elsewhere in Algar, certification decides who can take which jobs. Crews arriving by sea come looking for work or standing, and inland adventurers pass through between journeys.</p>

</details>

<details id="netwrights-and-mariners-guildhall">
<summary><strong>Netwrights' & Mariners' Guildhall</strong> — the guild of fisherfolk and shore workers</summary>

<p>The Netwrights' & Mariners' Guildhall stands near the working edge of town, between the shipyard docks and the road toward the fishery gate. It is a broad, practical building of weathered timber on stone footings, with doors wide enough to take bundled nets and crates of gear. Inside, the hall is divided between open meeting space and work areas, with long tables for tallying catch and settling accounts and side rooms of shared nets, lines, and tools kept for members in need. Notice boards list weather warnings, seasonal restrictions, crew assignments, and the current prices negotiated between merchants and buyers.</p>

<p>The guild represents fisherfolk and shore workers of every kind, including netmakers, dory crews, salters, smokers, and transport hands. It sets catch quotas, preservation standards, and safety measures, usually by firm consensus rather than decree. It is not a governing body, but it holds quiet authority, and it can influence supply, delay distribution, or press its concerns when conditions turn unsafe or unfair. Merchants, dock officials, and even Crown representatives take its voice seriously. At dawn and dusk the hall fills with crews coming or going, and for the people who work the sea it is the place where shared risk and shared labor hold the community together.</p>

</details>

<details id="runners-and-couriers-guildhouse">
<summary><strong>Runners' & Couriers' Guildhouse</strong> — the town's message and parcel service</summary>

<p>The Runners' & Couriers' Guildhouse sits on a busy stretch of road in southern Cairnellah between the Fishery Gate and the Wexford Gate, across from the airship dock and beside the Happy Clam Tavern. That puts it at a natural crossroads of sea, land, and sky traffic. The guild is the town's main message and light parcel service, handling everything from local deliveries to long-distance dispatches across Algar and beyond. Letters, contracts, small goods, and urgent papers travel by trained runner, mounted courier, or guarded escort depending on the delivery.</p>

<p>Inside, a spacious central hall serves as receiving and dispatch, with boards that track outgoing routes and expected arrivals and clerks who coordinate schedules. Adjacent rooms provide lodging for members between assignments and storage for parcels waiting to move. For those able to pay, the guild's resident mages can send messages by magical means, drastically cutting travel time, though such services are carefully managed and priced for urgent or high-value messages. Runners come and go at all hours and often celebrate their returns at the tavern next door. For adventurers the guild offers reliable communication, escort work, and a window onto news moving across the realm.</p>

</details>

## Inns and Taverns

<details id="red-wake-inn">
<summary><strong>Red Wake Inn</strong> — the loud, weathered sailors' inn</summary>

<p>The Red Wake Inn stands near the working edge of the docks, a weathered building of timber and stone painted red and faded in places by years of salt wind and sun. A linen banner hangs over the street and a chalked menu board stands by the door. The upper stories jut out over the street, and the patched woodwork shows long use rather than careful design. Inside it is loud and crowded, with long scarred tables and air thick with ale, salt, and damp rope.</p>

<p>A human proprietor keeps a firm but understated hand on the place, letting the mood run so long as it does not threaten the building or the business. Most of the service falls to a capable staff of gullfolk, quick and sharp-eyed on the crowded floor. The rooms above are sparse and used mostly by sailors on shore leave or travelers unwilling to pay for better. It is a good place to hear news from distant ports and rumors up and down the coast, though the news arrives with plenty of exaggeration, and those who listen carefully can find the truth under the noise.</p>

</details>

<details id="parkline-lodge">
<summary><strong>Parkline Lodge</strong> — the exclusive inn for wealthy travelers</summary>

<p>Parkline Lodge is an exclusive inn for wealthy travelers and discerning guests. The three-story building is dressed stone with polished brass accents, and its grounds are bordered by manicured greencap trees and clusters of exotic shrubs, a quiet contrast to the working harbor nearby. Inside, apartments and private rooms have fine furnishings and attentive service, and a small number of long-term residents keep permanent suites. Entry is not openly restricted, but guests are expected to behave accordingly.</p>

<p>The dining here is considered among the finest in Cairnellah, with a menu of local ingredients and imported fare served discreetly and efficiently. The lodge is no place of open commerce, but it has influence of its own, since contracts are quietly discussed and alliances formed beyond the notice of the docks and markets. For adventurers of means it offers comfort and privacy, and for those seeking opportunity it offers access, if they can gain entry.</p>

</details>

<details id="happy-clam-tavern">
<summary><strong>The Happy Clam Tavern</strong> — the first stop for travelers from the south</summary>

<p>The Happy Clam Tavern stands just inside the southern coastal gate, the Fishery Gate, across from the permanent market and just south of the airship dock. That makes it a natural first stop for travelers coming north on the road, and a handy gathering place for merchants, fishery workers, and adventurers. The building is sturdy and practical, with a broad common room, attached stables, and basic but clean rooms upstairs. Meals are simple and filling, drawn from the day's catch and local provisions.</p>

<p>A dragonblood innkeeper, who is also the main bartender, runs the tavern with a steady presence. House rules are enforced without fuss, and newcomers learn quickly that respect goes a long way. The crowd is mixed, with traders fresh off the road, fishmongers from the market, and adventurers looking for work or information, so news from inland routes meets word from the coast, and new arrivals can find their feet before moving deeper into town.</p>

</details>

<details id="wayfarers-rest-inn">
<summary><strong>Wayfarer's Rest Inn</strong> — the overland travelers' inn by the Wexford Gate</summary>

<p>The Wayfarer's Rest sits just inside the southern gate where the road from Wexford meets Cairnellah's walls, placed to catch the steady flow of caravans, couriers, and travelers on foot. It is built for practicality, a broad-fronted building with a stable yard, a covered entry, and a well-worn common room. An aelfaen proprietor keeps it, receiving guests without fuss, assigning rooms efficiently, and settling disputes before they grow. The rooms are modest but well kept, the meals are hot and simple, and the stables are tended well enough to make the inn a favorite of mounted parties and caravan handlers.</p>

<p>The crowd here is different from the coastal inns, and the talk turns to road conditions, forest routes, caravan schedules, and news from the inland settlements. Adventurers coming up from the south often gather information here first, or make their last preparations before setting out beyond the walls. It is not a place of intrigue, but one of steady passage, where journeys begin, end, or pause to gather strength.</p>

</details>

<details id="blue-whale-inn">
<summary><strong>Blue Whale Inn</strong> — the first lodging for travelers from the north and east</summary>

<p>The Blue Whale Inn stands in the northeast quarter, where the north coastal road meets the main inland route from Bridgeport, and it is often the first lodging travelers meet when entering town from that side. A painted wooden sign showing a cheerful blue whale cresting above stylized waves marks it from the road. It lies at the edge of a lower-income residential district but keeps a respectable, well-kept look, with clean, modest rooms and stables big enough for small caravans and mounted visitors.</p>

<p>The common room is the inn's best feature, lively without being unruly, with traders, travelers, locals, and a notable number of off-duty Crown soldiers from the nearby compound. Talk tends to be grounded and varied, and news from Bridgeport usually arrives here first. A halfling family owns and runs the inn, and their steady presence keeps it orderly and welcoming. It is solid, trusted, and a dependable place for adventurers to rest, gather information, and take stock.</p>

</details>

## Temples and Cloisters

<details id="temple-of-the-joyous-dance">
<summary><strong>Temple of the Joyous Dance</strong> — the marble temple of Merryn at the heart of town</summary>

<p>At the heart of Cairnellah stands the Temple of the Joyous Dance, a broad marble building dedicated to the Eternal Merryn. Its polished stone, open colonnades, and wide central floor make it as much a gathering place as a house of worship, and it draws residents and visitors alike to its constant rhythm of movement and celebration. Banners bearing Merryn's symbol, a twisting ribbon in primary colors set before a radiant sunburst, hang throughout. Regular gatherings invite everyone to dance regardless of background or belief, and Merryn asks little beyond participation. Healing, comfort, and other aid are offered freely, and donations to sustain the temple are welcome.</p>

<p>The clergy hold loosely structured services that blend ritual with celebration. To outsiders it can look chaotic, but the priests, acolytes, and wardens share the conviction that joy, movement, and connection are sacred in themselves. Noble court steps, lively rural reels, and the percussive styles of those shaped by the wilds all share the floor as equals. Trees and flowering plants surround the temple, a stable and broad turnaround serve the carriages that arrive on event evenings, and a rectory nearby houses the pryor and clergy. It is the spiritual center of Cairnellah and a living expression of its people.</p>

</details>

<details id="shrine-of-the-broken-coin">
<summary><strong>Shrine of the Broken Coin</strong> — the dark shrine of Bran, patron of small fortunes</summary>

<p>Near the eastern wall, among the town's residential districts, the Shrine of the Broken Coin rises in stark contrast to its surroundings. It is built of dark stone with narrow windows and steep lines, and has a somber, almost gothic presence, yet it is among the most visited temples in Cairnellah and open to anyone. It is dedicated to <a href="{{< relref "the-gods#bran" >}}">Bran of the Broken Coin</a>, an Ascendant of <a href="{{< relref "the-gods#toiy" >}}">Toiy</a>, and it serves people who live by uncertain fortune: laborers, dockhands, messengers, and travelers who come to start or end their day and ask for a small turn of luck, enough to find work, avoid misfortune, or just get through another day.</p>

<p>Inside, the space is broad and functional rather than ornate. Offerings of coins, tokens, and small personal items go into iron bowls or onto worn stone surfaces, and there are no great sermons, only a steady stream of people who arrive, pause, make an offering, and leave. The clergy are practical and grounded, and their blessings are brief, given with the understanding that luck is only ever nudged. Boards on the walls list day work, caravan needs, and ship postings, so the shrine doubles as an informal exchange for small opportunities. It is not a gambling hall, but small rituals of chance are part of its practice, and it is said to be the best place in town to roll the dice on a future fortune.</p>

</details>

<details id="sanctum-of-the-cold-embrace">
<summary><strong>Sanctum of the Cold Embrace</strong> — the blue granite sanctum of Cora, by the docks</summary>

<p>Near the western docks, across the street from the Adventurers' Guild and just east of the Netwrights' & Mariners' Guildhall, stands the Sanctum of the Cold Embrace, a place of quiet reverence for people who live by the sea. It is built of blue granite that darkens when wet, and its smooth, austere lines reflect the beauty and indifference of the primordial <a href="{{< relref "the-gods#cora" >}}">Cora</a>, whose domain is the vast and unforgiving waters. The sanctum is modest in size but one of the busiest in town. Sailors, fishers, and dockworkers pass through at all hours, offering coin, salt, or small tokens before they put out to sea and returning to give thanks for a safe arrival.</p>

<p>Inside it is cool and dim, with the sound of water everywhere. Shallow channels run along the floor, fed by a constant flow that gathers in a central basin and drains away, a symbol of the endless cycle of tide and current, and offerings are placed along the basin's edge or cast into the water. The clergy of Cora keep the sanctum with quiet discipline and perform minimal, deliberate rites that acknowledge rather than petition. A blessing might be a touch of water, a murmured phrase, or a moment of stillness, and it promises recognition of the risk, not protection from it. Crews gather here with their hands on the wet stone before they leave, and others return in silence or relief.</p>

</details>

<details id="cloister-of-the-open-hand">
<summary><strong>Cloister of the Open Hand</strong> — the walled monastic compound in the south of town</summary>

<p>About a block north of the Wexford Gate, just east of the airship dock, stands the Cloister of the Open Hand, a walled monastic compound of the Order of the Open Hand. Inside the stone boundary are four main structures, an administrative hall with a library and scriptorium, barracks, a mess, and a modest auditorium, all west of a spacious yard used for training and meditation. The Order placed it within the movement and noise of the city on purpose, because its followers believe discipline is tested in distraction, not silence, and the sounds of dockwork, caravans, and airships carry over the walls.</p>

<p>The hierarchy is clear but understated. The Master of the Open Hand oversees doctrine, training, and the Order's conduct in Cairnellah, Guides handle instruction and discipline, and those in training are called Seekers, who live, work, and train inside. Days are structured but not rigid, with physical training, meditation, shared meals, and chores. The teaching stresses awareness, restraint, and intentional action, and the idea that strength lies in choosing when and how to act. Members are sometimes seen outside the walls, where they observe, help, or step in when needed. They are not enforcers or guards, but the people of Cairnellah notice them when a conflict threatens to escalate, and they act to restore balance, not to dominate.</p>

</details>

## Gates, Roads, and Trails

<details id="fishery-gate">
<summary><strong>Fishery Gate</strong> — the southwest coastal gate</summary>

<p>The Fishery Gate faces southwest and is the southernmost armored gate in Cairnellah's coastal defenses. The seaward walls here are stone, and they give way eastward to the timber landward defenses. Twin towers flank the iron-banded doors, which are backed by a heavy portcullis, and each tower mounts a ballista that can be trained seaward or along the shore road.</p>

<p>Traffic through the gate is mostly known locals, such as fishers, farmers, and regular laborers, along with people on business for Lord Sand, whose manor lies fifty miles to the south. Travel beyond that point is effectively cut off by the near-impassable ruins of Nereth on the contaminated Gold River, some seventy miles away, so foreign arrivals are uncommon and watched closely.</p>

</details>

<details id="shore-road">
<summary><strong>Shore Road</strong> — the coastal road south to Sand Manor</summary>

<p>The Shore Road is a stone-laid coastal road built at great expense by the Crown, running south from Cairnellah to Sand Manor. Crown authority and local guild labor maintain it, and it is patrolled regularly, which makes safe passage out of what would otherwise be a harsh and exposed route. Near town it is lively, lined with fisheries, boathouses, and clusters of fisher homes, nets, and drying racks. Within a mile these give way to open shoreline and a broad, windswept plain.</p>

<p>For several miles the landscape is stark. The white expanse of the Sea of Zephyrys lies to the west and low dunes of coarse grass and scrub rise to the east, and the wind piles sand across the road and scatters shells and driftwood that must be cleared regularly. Roughly twenty miles south stands a cluster of weathered huts used seasonally by oyster divers. Some are licensed by the Crown and others are not, and the place has a reputation for both opportunity and quiet disputes, since the offshore beds can yield valuable pearls. On clear days the dunes give a glimpse of the Wexwood Forest inland, and past the divers the road runs in long, lonely stretches to Sand Manor, where the Baron's presence returns order and habitation.</p>

</details>

<details id="wexford-gate">
<summary><strong>Wexford Gate</strong> — the landward gate of the southern wall</summary>

<p>The Wexford Gate is set into the timber-built southern wall and is the main landward entry for traffic from the inland road. Two wooden watchtowers frame it, and it is built for steady use, not display. Crown soldiers keep a regular guard rotation and inspect incoming wagons, livestock, and foot traffic. Nearby farms, mills, and outlying homes use it constantly, along with traders and travelers coming from Wexford and beyond.</p>

<p>It is not as heavily fortified as the coastal gates, but it is a vital point of entry and control. The double wooden doors are secured each night, and spotters in the towers watch the road and fields. By day the area is active but orderly, with goods and people moving steadily between Cairnellah and the inland routes that sustain it.</p>

</details>

<details id="hot-springs-road">
<summary><strong>Hot Springs Road</strong> — the inland road to the springs and Wexford</summary>

<p>This hard-packed wagon road is Cairnellah's main inland route through the Wexford Gate. It heads southeast from town through open farmland and grassland to the edge of the Wexwood Forest, where it bends east beneath the trees. Along this stretch lies a well-known series of naturally heated pools called the Hot Springs. Their mineral-rich waters are said to be restorative and draw locals, travelers, and the occasional noble looking for rest, and simple facilities and seasonal vendors have grown up around them, though the place remains largely natural.</p>

<p>Beyond the springs the road continues through the forest to the town of Wexford, serving as a steady artery for trade, travel, and communication between the two settlements. East of Wexford lie Riffel Station and the Queen's Road, which lead to the interior of Algar.</p>

</details>

<details id="east-gate">
<summary><strong>East Gate</strong> — the busiest landward gate</summary>

<p>Like the Wexford Gate in purpose but far more substantial, the East Gate is set into the timber-built wall and is the town's main link to the Bridgeport Road. It is the busiest of the landward gates and handles most of the commercial traffic between the freehold and the coast. It is larger, taller, and more heavily reinforced than the southern gate, with a raised walkway over the portcullis and wagon gates from which guards oversee traffic. Elevated platforms with ballista flank it, commanding the road approaches and deterring organized threats.</p>

<p>Crown soldiers are always on duty here, inspecting wagons, assessing tariffs, and reviewing manifests for incoming caravans. The gate runs at a steady rhythm from dawn to nightfall, and local farms and mills use it as well as long-distance trade. Its size reflects both the economic importance of the Bridgeport route and the need to defend one of the town's most vital approaches.</p>

</details>

<details id="bridgeport-road">
<summary><strong>Bridgeport Road</strong> — the wagon road to Bridgeport</summary>

<p>The Bridgeport Road is a well-traveled wagon road linking the royal town of Cairnellah with the freehold city of Bridgeport, Algar's major river and seaport. It runs nearly straight for about eighty miles through fertile farmland, connecting smaller holdings, mills, and waypoints, among them the hamlet of Skiff. It carries a steady flow of trade, with wagons moving goods between inland producers and Cairnellah's docks, and travelers, messengers, and caravans use it too. Crown patrols and local wardens keep a visible presence to keep the route safe.</p>

<p>Cairnellah is far smaller than Bridgeport, but its coastal position, its shipyards, and its closeness to the Bimbot Lands draw notable attention, so traffic on the road is heavier than the town's size would suggest. Merchants, couriers, guild factors, and Crown riders pass along it through much of the year, making it a vital artery between the freehold and the western coast.</p>

</details>

<details id="north-gate">
<summary><strong>North Gate</strong> — the quiet gate to the northern shore</summary>

<p>The North Gate mirrors the Wexford Gate in design and is set into the northern edge of the timber-built wall. It is less prominent than the East Gate, but it is a steady point of access for local movement between the town and the shoreline of Godsfall Bay. Two wooden watchtowers frame it, the double doors are secured at night, and a rotating detail of Crown soldiers watches over it. Traffic is lighter and more familiar, mostly residents, laborers, and small-scale traders going between town and the surrounding properties.</p>

<p>Beyond the gate, a coastal road follows the curve of the bay and connects a scattering of homes, mills, and both public and private piers. Some trade passes through, but most of the activity is local and tied to work along the water. The gate lacks the heavy fortification of the main entries, but it is an essential if quiet part of the town's circulation.</p>

</details>

<details id="bayshore-trail">
<summary><strong>Bayshore Trail</strong> — the horse path along Godsfall Bay</summary>

<p>A few miles past Cairnellah the coastal road narrows into a well-worn horse path that follows the shore of Godsfall Bay for nearly seventy miles. It is less formal than the Crown's main roads, winding along the water past scattered cottages, small piers, and quiet stretches of shoreline shaped by tide and weather. It is used mostly by fishers, messengers, and travelers who want a more direct if less maintained route between coastal settlements, and parts of it are subject to flooding, erosion, or drifting sand, so it takes care and familiarity to follow safely.</p>

<p>The trail ends at Peregrine Point, a small hamlet with a lighthouse at the mouth of Pell's Harbor, the seaport of Bridgeport. It is not a primary trade route, but it is a steady coastal link for those who prefer the shore to the main road.</p>

</details>
