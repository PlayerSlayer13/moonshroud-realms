# Session 05 — A Door in the Dark, a Name Given Away, and the Reason Fernoir Built a Wall
*Frostwatch, Day 4, Year 700AG — continued*

---

## The Flooded Tunnel

With the tunnel ahead clearly underwater, the party had to figure out what to do about it.
**Bo-Bo** climbed along the ceiling far enough to confirm it: fully submerged, no telling how
far it went before opening back up, if it opened back up at all. **Ender** wanted absolutely
no part of getting wet, let alone swimming blind through a flooded tunnel with no idea what
lived in the water or where it came out. Nobody talked him out of it.

They decided to backtrack instead. Ender took point and led them, without a wrong turn, back
to the spot where Vail had gone chasing after the second beast.

---

## A Boundary That Doesn't Sit Still

On the way, something odd surfaced. **Bo-Bo** was sure the route back felt longer than the
route out; **Vail** was just as sure it felt shorter. Vail knew, on some level, there was a
reason for that, but between the rats and whatever else might still be down there, she
couldn't quite pin it down.

It nagged at her enough that she stopped and pushed on it deliberately — and pulled up
something old, something closer to instinct than memory: they'd been skirting the edge of a
boundary between Orb and the plane of Haelo. Boundaries like that don't sit still. They move,
the way small waves move along a shoreline, ebbing and flowing without ever quite settling.
That was the reason the same stretch of tunnel had felt like two different lengths to two
different people walking it at the same time. She laid it out for the others as best she
could.

---

## Thistle

They pushed on, working their way toward the western stretch they'd been trying to reach the
whole time, until the tunnels opened into a junction — five separate passages meeting at a
wider, more open space, though the ceiling still sat only about eight feet up. Off to one side
was a door, the first one they'd seen down here. Ajar, with a flicker of candlelight coming
from whatever was behind it.

Vail found her own way in and slipped through first, and whoever — whatever — was on the
other side noticed her almost the instant she arrived, and asked, politely, if she'd invite
her friends in too.

Questions followed, mostly from Vail, and the answers she got back were vague but never
dishonest. Their host gave his name as **Thistle**. He knew the name of the creature they'd
killed earlier without being told — called it an **Urco**. He described himself as Fae, an
observer who'd been watching over this stretch of the unclaimed reaches longer than he cared
to put a number to. And somewhere in the conversation, he named an organization none of them
had heard of before: the **Fernoir**.

---

## The Bargain

A bargain got struck. **Alden** told Thistle how the Urco had gone down, and in exchange,
Thistle agreed to walk them to what he called the weak spot. Along the way, they passed
several walled-off side tunnels, and Vail wanted to know why the Fernoir had bothered sealing
them. That cost another bargain — a name of real importance, hers to give and his to keep, one
she wouldn't be able to say again until she was back up on the surface. She thought about it,
and gave it to him.

In return, Thistle told them why, laying it out plain:

*"Fernoir didn't wall this off because something lives here," he said. "Things live
everywhere. They don't care about that. They walled it off because their instruments stopped
agreeing with each other. Two surveyors, same corridor, same hour — one measures it forty
paces, the other measures it thirty-six. A compass that points true everywhere else in this
city spins lazy circles the moment it crosses into the Reaches. A man goes in whistling a
tune, comes out an hour later humming a different one entirely, swears he never stopped."*

*"Nothing dangerous, you understand. Nothing that bit or burned or demanded a toll. Just…
unreliable. And Fernoir, bless their tidy little hearts, cannot abide unreliable. A ledger
that doesn't balance is worse to them than a ledger that's been robbed. So rather than write
down 'this section does not behave the way sections are supposed to behave' — rather than
admit that — they walled it. More than once. Called it not worth the effort, filed it under
sealed, and went back to corridors that stayed where they were put."*

*"That's the why. Exactly as it happened, exactly as it means. This ground doesn't agree with
itself the way the rest of the city does. Make of that what you will."*

From there, he led them to the walled-off stretch holding the weak spot — pointed it out, and
was upfront that getting through it wouldn't be quick or easy, but that this was the one place
the Fernoir hadn't sealed properly.

As Thistle turned to head back to his hiding place, Vail told him she'd come visit again
sometime. He seemed genuinely pleased by that.

---

## Notes

- **Thistle** — a Fae observer encountered in a junction chamber deep in the sewers, watching
over "the unclaimed reaches" for longer than he cares to count. Friendly, cryptic, deals in
bargains rather than straight answers.
- **The Fernoir** — an organization the party had not heard named before this session.
According to Thistle, they seal off sections of the underground where measurements and
instruments stop behaving consistently — not out of danger, but out of an apparent
intolerance for anything that doesn't add up cleanly.
- Vail gave Thistle a name of personal significance as payment for information. She won't be
able to speak it again until she's back on the surface.
- The party has been led to a weak spot in one of Fernoir's walled-off sections — confirmed
accessible, but not confirmed easy.
- The creature killed in Session 4 was identified by Thistle as an **Urco**.
