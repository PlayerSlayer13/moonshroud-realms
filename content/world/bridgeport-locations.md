# Bridgeport Locations

Notable districts, landmarks, and establishments across Bridgeport, organized by the two
halves of the city the Godsfall River divides. Click any entry to expand it.

---

## West Bridgeport

<details id="saltstone-square">
<summary><strong>Saltstone Square</strong> — the civic and social heart of the southern district</summary>

<p>Saltstone Square has its own full entry — see the <a href="/world/saltstone-square/">Saltstone
Square</a> page for the Strangled Goat, the Royal Prince Inn, the Dragonline Lodge, and the
square's role as Bridgeport's busiest commercial and social crossroads.</p>

</details>

<details id="queensway-avenue">
<summary><strong>Queensway Avenue</strong> — Bridgeport's principal north-south boulevard</summary>

<p>Bridgeport's main artery, a wide and well-maintained boulevard lined with guildhalls,
merchant houses, and artisan storefronts. Traffic flows along its length constantly, from
the docks up toward the hills. Lanterns maintained by the Chandlers' Council keep it lit
well into the night, making it one of the safer routes through the city after dark.</p>

<p>Queensway crosses the Seaward Span northward and continues on as the main road toward the
Sanctum Hills.</p>

</details>

<details id="the-racket">
<summary><strong>The Racket</strong> — a crowded, close-knit working district</summary>

<p>Bridgeport's most crowded and rough-edged neighborhood — a maze of leaning tenements,
patched roofs, and packed dirt streets standing in stark contrast to the cobblestones found
elsewhere in the city. Noise, color, and constant motion define the quarter.</p>

<p>At its heart stands the Shrine of the Humble Virtues, a broad temple-like hall dedicated to
a triad of Ascendants embodying simple joy, everyday luck, and steadfast hope. Its doors are
always open. The Racket is poor in coin and notably rich in solidarity — outsiders who
mistake its rough appearance for weakness tend to find out otherwise.</p>

</details>

<details id="freegate">
<summary><strong>Freegate</strong> — the southern gate into the city</summary>

<p>The southern edge of old Bridgeport, where a broad stone arch flanked by watchtowers opens
toward the farmland beyond. Road-toll inspectors and agricultural assessors work from the
gatehouse. The name endures from the Freehold's early days, when Bridgeport resisted noble
tariffs and insisted this entrance remain open and untaxed. Locals still treat the arch as a
symbolic threshold — the point where the city's noise falls away and open country begins.</p>

</details>

<details id="drydock-quay">
<summary><strong>Drydock Quay</strong> — the Inner Harbor's salvage and repair yards</summary>

<p>A long stretch of the Inner Harbor given over to salvage work and ship repairs. Once a
bustling center of construction, it now handles essential maintenance — the rhythmic creak
of ropes and clang of tools echoing across the water as crews labor to keep the city's
vessels seaworthy. It carries a faint sense of faded glory, as though the timbers remember a
time when entire fleets were launched from these docks.</p>

<details id="seaglass-tower">
<summary><strong>Seaglass Tower</strong> — the militia's harbor watchpoint</summary>

<p>A compact stone fort crowned by a distinctive green-blue glass lantern dome, standing on a
narrow peninsula overlooking Pell's Harbor. Operated by the city militia, it serves as
Bridgeport's primary watchpoint for approaching vessels, its lantern guiding ships through
fog and night. From its battlements, the entire sweep of Pell's Harbor can be surveyed in a
single turn of the head.</p>

<details id="riverbend-mills">
<summary><strong>Riverbend Mills</strong> — the city's milling and lumber works</summary>

<p>Sits along the banks of Stonewash Creek, a fast, cold run of water descending from the
Livingstone Mountains before emptying into the Inner Harbor. The complex contains
wheelhouses, saw frames, drying racks, and storage sheds. The air is thick with the mixed
scents of cut wood and ground grain, and wagons come and go at all hours.</p>

</details>

<details id="northwood-stands">
<summary><strong>Northwood Stands</strong> — the woodland bordering the city's northern edge</summary>

<p>The dense woodland bordering Bridgeport's northern edge — rich in game, timber, herbs,
and hidden trails. A few licensed trappers work its outskirts, and most avoid the deeper
groves without experienced guides. Hunters speak of faint blue lights drifting between the
trees and of tracks that appear and vanish again.</p>

<p>Roads leave Bridgeport in two directions from here: northwest into the Livingstone
Mountains through Hayden Gap, and northeast through rolling highlands toward the Sanctum
Hills.</p>

</details>

<details id="seaward-span">
<summary><strong>Seaward Span</strong> — the western bridge across the Godsfall</summary>

<p>The western of Bridgeport's two great bridges, a broad, gracefully arched structure built
high enough to allow riverboats and barge traffic to pass beneath without halting the city's
constant flow of trade. Its stonework is sturdy but elegant, one of the most recognizable
features of the Godsfall River crossing.</p>

<p>Watchposts staffed by the city militia stand at either end, inspecting large carts,
monitoring unusual cargo, and keeping order along the busy route linking the southern civic
quarter to the districts north of the river. On clear mornings the view from the span is one
of the finest in the city, with Pell's Harbor stretching westward in a sweep of ships, masts,
and shimmering water.</p>

</details>

<details id="fort-royal">
<summary><strong>Fort Royal</strong> — the Crown's garrison</summary>

<p>Fort Royal stands as the Crown's foremost military stronghold in Bridgeport, a stone
bastion overlooking Pell's Harbor and unmistakably separate from the Freehold's civic
defenses. Staffed exclusively by a full regiment of the Algarian Crown Guard, the fort
answers directly to royal command in Knightsbridge rather than to the Council of Trades. No
Bridgeport militia serves within its walls, though a formal liaison keeps diplomatic
coordination between the Crown Guard and the Council running smoothly.</p>

<p>From this outpost, Crown Guard patrols radiate out across the region — north toward the
Sanctum Hills, east along the Godsfall River valley, south toward the routes approaching the
Wexwood Forest, and west along the coastline. These patrols enforce royal authority,
safeguard the trade roads, and respond to threats beyond the mandate or resources of
Bridgeport's own civic forces.</p>

<p>The fort is commanded by Colonel Alastair Thorneveil, a disciplined and tactically minded
officer whose allegiance to the Crown is absolute. Under his leadership the regiment
maintains immaculate drill yards, vigilant walls, and a constant state of readiness — a
standing reminder that Bridgeport's Freehold, though autonomous, remains under the
Monarch's watch.</p>

</details>

## East Bridgeport

<details id="ledger-house">
<summary><strong>Ledger House</strong> — a private mercantile club and counting house</summary>

<p>Occupying a prominent corner lot on the edge of the commercial districts, across the
avenue from the Temple of Veyruun and only a short walk from the Grand Exchange, Ledger
House presents itself as one of Bridgeport's more respectable mercantile institutions. The
substantial stone-and-timber guildhall makes a convenient meeting place for merchants,
factors, ship captains, investors, and brokers whose livelihoods depend on contracts,
cargoes, and commerce.</p>

<p>Officially, Ledger House operates as a private commercial club, counting house, brokerage,
and warehouse consortium. Its ground floor holds a well-appointed dining room, lounges,
meeting chambers, card rooms, and private offices where members conduct business away from
the noise of the public markets. The upper floors house clerks, accountants, records
specialists, and brokers managing a vast web of contracts, cargo manifests, shipping
schedules, and financial agreements. Behind the main structure stand several secure
warehouses and carriage yards that receive deliveries day and night — as constant and
unremarkable a sight in Bridgeport as paperwork and cargo have always been.</p>

</details>
