# Trellen Gap

Some four hundred miles east of Bridgeport, deep within the Duchy of Rhundel's vast Great
Forest, Trellen Gap is a frontier hamlet of roughly one hundred fifty souls — humans, aelfaen,
and halflings who have carved out a life in a natural corridor where two wooded ridges pull
apart just enough to let a clearing, a few roads, and a settlement take hold.

## The Great Forest

The Great Forest itself is immense, running from the Camiain Hills in the north and east to
the World Holes in the south and the Plains of Bounty to the west. Trellen Gap sits deep
within that expanse, far from the ducal seat of Rhundel, bound by its laws but shaped by its
own distance from them — independence born of isolation rather than defiance.

Three roads connect the hamlet to the wider world: one north toward Hillview in the East
Camiain Hills, one west toward Foam Lake and the hidden centaur settlement of G'ryn beyond it,
and one south through dense woodland to the Chasm River crossing, roughly twenty miles out,
just before it meets the smaller Aelthrane River that runs close by the hamlet itself.

## Governance

Trellen Gap is governed by a reeve appointed under the authority of the Duchy of Rhundel,
responsible for order, local disputes, militia coordination, and tax collection. In a
settlement this small, governance is personal rather than bureaucratic — the reeve is expected
to be visible, approachable, and fair, conducting business from a front room that doubles as
an office rather than from behind any real seat of power.

## Defense

Palisade walls of sharpened hardwood logs ring the hamlet, each gate stocked with spare timber
and bundled arrows ready at a moment's notice. When horns sound, a well-practiced volunteer
militia of all ages gathers along the walls, joined by hunters, woodcutters, and druids
retreating from the forest to the safety of the settlement. Survival here depends less on
strength than on vigilance and shared responsibility.

## Economy

The hamlet's economy is simple but vital: rest and resupply for traders and travelers moving
through the forest, with timber, game, and forest goods providing modest but steady income.
Despite its isolation, Trellen Gap stands as a dependable waypoint in an unforgiving
wilderness, where a warm meal and a sturdy roof are never taken for granted.

## The Aelthrane Ruins

Between Cord Creek and the banks of the Aelthrane River lie the weathered remains of an
ancient aelfaen settlement — moss-covered arches, fractured foundations, and old wells that
have long drawn curiosity. Children play among the outer stones in daylight, but the hamlet's
elders forbid excavation or deeper exploration, warning that the ruins remember more than they
reveal.
