# Dalraven Locations

The roads, gates, docks, halls, and farmsteads of Dalraven and its immediate surroundings, grouped by where a traveler would meet them. Click any entry to expand it. ## Roads and Water

<details id="highbreaker-road">
<summary><strong>Highbreaker Road</strong> — the mountain road from Bridgeport</summary>

<p>This mountain road comes down from Galross Pass in the Algar Mountains, after climbing through Hayden Gap in the Livingstone Range out of the Godsfall valley. Wagons of any size cannot manage it, but mounted riders and travelers on foot sometimes take it between Dalraven and Bridgeport, except in winter.</p>

<p>In practice only experienced adventurers, or those traveling with hired protection, make the crossing routinely. Weather, rockslides, and opportunistic predators make the journey as dangerous as any opponent.</p>

</details>

<details id="river-road">
<summary><strong>River Road</strong> — the principal road inland</summary>

<p>The River Road leaves Dalraven and follows the north bank of the Raven River inland. It passes the tree-lined edge of Vayne Manor and several prosperous farmsteads, and the surface is firm enough for wool carts, grain wagons, and modest river trade.</p>

<p>It is Dalraven's main road into the interior and leads on to the bugbear settlement of Gor-Uk, the foothills called the Numps of Mar, and the Hen's Teeth mountains. Crown patrols ride it when circumstances demand, though oversight thins the further one gets from the walls.</p>

</details>

<details id="steading-road">
<summary><strong>Steading Road</strong> — the farm road toward Spelman's Bane</summary>

<p>The Steading Road leaves through the Farm Gate and serves the homesteads and farms northeast of town. For about the first mile it is a decent farm road that wagons can use.</p>

<p>Beyond the last fields it narrows to a rough trail leading into the blighted expanse called Spelman's Bane. The Crown does not patrol it regularly, and anyone who passes the final farmstead does so knowing they have left Dalraven's protection behind.</p>

</details>

<details id="coastal-road">
<summary><strong>Coastal Road</strong> — north along the sea</summary>

<p>The Coastal Road leaves through the North Gate and runs along the sea's edge, crossing tidal mudflats and open moor. Over the low stretches, logs laid under packed gravel keep the worst of the ground from swallowing wagons.</p>

<p>It bends northeast toward Gull Point, then curves back northwest across the windswept reaches of Cravenmoor to Millesh Bay and the northern heart of the duchy. It is exposed to wind and sea, but it is maintained and patrolled by the Crown, and it carries trade, tax wagons, and official correspondence between Dalraven and the outlying settlements.</p>

</details>

<details id="godsfall-bay">
<summary><strong>Godsfall Bay</strong> — the water off the docks</summary>

<p>The tidal waters beyond Dalraven's docks are the northern reach of Godsfall Bay, a vast inlet of the Sea of Zephyrys, the ocean that marks Algar's western frontier. Dalraven sits on the bay's upper curve, and the same bay receives the Godsfall River far to the south, where Bridgeport stands as Algar's principal port.</p>

<p>The bay supports Dalraven's fisheries, trade, and modest seafaring. Its tides are steady, its shoals shift but are known to local pilots, and the horizon is always open.</p>

</details>


## Gates

<details id="galross-gate">
<summary><strong>Galross Gate</strong> — the toll gate facing the mountains</summary>

<p>Galross Gate is one of two heavy toll gates into Dalraven and faces the mountain road toward Bridgeport. Town guards in the local lord's livery, three black boar heads on fields of gold and purple, man it. Visitors pay a modest but consistently enforced toll, and heavily armed groups or mercenary companies are often charged more under the name of a security levy.</p>

<p>Two guards normally stand outside, while two more sit in the tower above, one as lookout and the other working the winch and wheel that move the reinforced wagon doors. By day a smaller pedestrian door in one leaf stays open, so people can pass without unbarring the main entry.</p>

</details>

<details id="north-gate">
<summary><strong>North Gate</strong> — the busiest gate for ducal trade</summary>

<p>The North Gate guards the coastal road coming in from Cravenmoor and the rest of the duchy. Galross Gate links the town to Bridgeport and the south, but the North Gate carries more of the ducal trade and taxation. Goods, messages, and levies moving between Dalraven and Millesh Bay, Dunhaven, and Bulger's Bay all pass beneath its arch.</p>

<p>It is heavily built and meant to be seen, flanked by watchtowers with sweeping views of the road and open moor. Traffic rivals the southern approach, with supply wagons, drovers, fish traders, messengers, and ducal agents arriving daily. Tolls are assessed on non-locals, and paperwork is checked with particular care, with frequent oversight from town officials and the Crown garrison. Because the road crosses wide exposed stretches, the gate also works as an early warning post, and riders heading north often carry sealed letters.</p>

</details>

<details id="farm-gate">
<summary><strong>Farm Gate</strong> — the northeastern exit</summary>

<p>The Farm Gate is Dalraven's northeastern exit, where the main road through town and over the Raven River continues out toward scattered farms and open country. It is modest compared to Galross and North Gates but essential to daily life.</p>

<p>The road beyond supports small homesteads that have grown up outside the walls, along with working fields and grazing plots, and it is also the most direct route toward the blighted region known as Spelman's Bane. A pair of small watchtowers bracket the gate, though normally only one is manned, with a single guard at ground level. No toll is collected from locals, and in living memory no unexpected caravan has come from this direction. A reinforced barricade can be rolled into place at dusk or in troubled times. Where the North Gate faces the duchy and Galross faces the mountains, the Farm Gate faces the unknown.</p>

</details>

<details id="orchard-gate">
<summary><strong>Orchard Gate</strong> — the farm gate south of the river</summary>

<p>Orchard Gate faces east along the south bank of the Raven River and serves the local road to the farms and orchards south of the river. It is built for daily movement more than defense, with a reinforced timber barricade that can be rolled into place and secured at night or during unrest.</p>

<p>Two modest watchtowers bracket it, but ordinarily only the riverside one is staffed, its lookout watching both the riverbanks and the orchard road. A single guard greets returning farmers and wagons. Inspections are light, and known tax-paying locals bringing in produce, livestock, or tools pay no toll.</p>

</details>

<details id="river-gate">
<summary><strong>River Gate</strong> — the western inland gate</summary>

<p>The River Gate is Dalraven's western inland entrance, where the river road bends toward the Raven River and continues upstream into the duchy's interior. It leads to scattered farms, the bugbear settlement of Gor-Uk, the foothills called the Numps of Mar, and eventually the Hen's Teeth mountains.</p>

<p>Traffic is steady but mostly local, with farmers, trappers, and riverfolk passing with little ceremony. Foreign caravans are rare, and there is no standing toll, though one may be charged to unfamiliar traders from upriver. A wooden watchtower anchors the end of the eastern town wall, and a second tower beside a narrow town pier watches the river channel. At night, reinforced wooden obstacles are rolled across the road and the pier access, making a defensible choke point without permanent gates. Those who come this way often carry news from lands less predictable than sea or mountain.</p>

</details>


## The Waterfront

<details id="town-docks">
<summary><strong>Town Docks</strong> — the waterfront</summary>

<p>The docks run the length of the main town along a narrow strip between the coastal road and the Sea of Zephyrys: a staggered line of timber wharves and practical warehouses. Some piers suit only small coastal craft and fishing boats, while longer ones take broader traders when the weather allows. The harbor is modest but active. Flat-bottomed boats, fishing smacks, and shallow-draft traders fill the slips, and larger ships anchor offshore and unload by lighter, guided by crews who know the shifting shallows.</p>

<p>Long, low warehouses behind the wharves store grain, salted fish, cider barrels, wool, timber, and outbound goods. Two larger buildings anchor the district. The Livestock House is a broad timber building with a fenced yard for sheep, goats, cattle, and draft animals awaiting shipment. The Excise Hall is a sturdier stone-faced building that serves as customs office, counting house, and passenger registry, where cargo is logged, tolls are assessed, manifests stamped, and passage on coastal craft arranged. The Dockworkers' Guild keeps things efficient, and coin passes through official hands on its way to the Moot Hall vaults. It is a rough, noisy place, and the wider world brushes close against Dalraven's walls here.</p>

</details>

<details id="dalraven-fishery">
<summary><strong>Dalraven Fishery</strong> — where the catch comes in</summary>

<p>At the northern end of the docks stands the Fishery, a broad timber complex where the day's catch is hauled ashore, sorted, cleaned, and distributed. From first light until midmorning the yard churns with activity, as boats unload into shallow carts that roll straight beneath long open sheds. Inside, rows of tables are used to gut, scale, and portion fish, and fresh catch goes quickly to fishmongers, taverns, and market stalls before the noon bell. Whatever is left is graded for transport or preservation, and offal and small fish are collected for fertilizer, feed, or bait.</p>

<p>Unlike the Saltworks, which cures fish in bulk for export, the Fishery handles intake and immediate sales, though a small drying yard and smoking shed handle short-term preservation. It operates under the Fishermen's Compact, a guild-recognized body of boat captains and net crews, with elected stewards who coordinate closely with the town excise officers. At dawn it smells of brine and scales, and by evening the yard is washed down and nearly quiet, with only gulls and the clatter of net-mending. In Dalraven the Fishery is not merely an industry. It is the town's pulse.</p>

</details>

<details id="blackshore-saltworks">
<summary><strong>Blackshore Saltworks</strong> — brine pans and smokehouses</summary>

<p>Blackshore Saltworks is a broad, low complex of timber sheds, brick ovens, brine pans, and fenced drying yards. In the main hall, iron cauldrons simmer brine over steady fires, while adjoining smokehouses cure fish, eel, and pork brought down from inland farms, with rafters heavy with drying strips and netted haunches.</p>

<p>The air carries a constant mix of brine, oak smoke, and sea wind, noticeable across town on damp mornings but not foul. It has become part of Dalraven's identity, a sign of industry and sustenance. The works let the town export preserved fish and smoked meats in good times, and keep patrols, fishing crews, and farmsteads supplied through lean months. It employs dockhands, seasonal laborers, and a small cadre of experienced briners who guard their timing and methods closely. Fires burn almost constantly, and a watch is kept both for accidents and for thieves trying to skim untaxed salt.</p>

</details>

<details id="gull-and-gaff">
<summary><strong>Gull &amp; Gaff Tavern</strong> — the liveliest house on the waterfront</summary>

<p>The Gull &amp; Gaff stands at the corner where the coastal road meets the main street from the bridge, directly across from the docks, and is Dalraven's most animated waterfront house. Its painted sign shows a gull perched indignantly on a ship's gaff spar and swings restlessly in the sea wind. Sailors, dockhands, laborers, and townsfolk crowd the floor, conversation runs loud, tankards are poured quickly, and disputes rarely last long. The food is filling, the ale dependable, and the air thick with brine and smoke drifting from the Saltworks.</p>

<p>The second floor offers semi-private rooms divided by timber partitions, and the top floor holds larger communal bunks favored by deckhands and overland travelers wanting a cheaper berth. A spacious stable yard faces the main street from the bridge, and Reeth "Rook" Kinoo, who runs the house, usually keeps a few horses fit for overland travel. He rents only to those he judges reliable and takes a hefty deposit, and anyone who fails his test of trustworthiness is simply told the animals are spoken for.</p>

</details>

<details id="grey-ghost-hammersmiths">
<summary><strong>Grey Ghost Hammersmiths</strong> — the town's armorer and weaponsmith</summary>

<p>This broad timber-and-stone building stands across from the docks beside the Gull &amp; Gaff and serves as Dalraven's main armorer and weaponsmith. Its double doors are often thrown open by day, letting the ring of hammer on steel spill into the street. The shopfront looks more like a warehouse than a boutique, with racks of plain weapons on reinforced beams, rows of shields, and barrels of bolts and bundled arrow shafts near the door.</p>

<p>The forge hall lies at the back, large and well ventilated, and Tak Grey-Ghost, a taciturn and exacting Ursinor, works the anvil there. He repairs damaged armor and weapons, ordinary or enchanted, with equal care. His twin brother Gab minds the shopfront, as approachable as his enormous size allows, and bargains fairly. The twins share a surname unusual among Ursinor, having chosen Grey-Ghost together after completing their naming quest side by side.</p>

</details>


## The Heart of Town

<details id="dalraven-market">
<summary><strong>Dalraven Market</strong> — a triangle of shops under sailcloth</summary>

<p>The market fills a broad open space near the center of town, built as a triangle. Three rows of open-faced timber shops form the outer edge, each angled inward toward a shared central court where stalls and temporary booths go up on market days. Heavy sailcloth canopies are rigged along the beams so workers can pull canvas over the court when squalls roll in, and in stronger gales the covers are struck and the outer shop fronts shuttered. The design reflects generations of adaptation to Dalraven's volatile weather.</p>

<p>Despite the town's size, the market offers a surprisingly varied selection through the year. Fishing trade and occasional coastal vessels bring in salt, spices, lamp oil, canvas, tools, and manufactured goods, while cider, salted fish, wool, leather, and timber goods leave for Millesh Bay and farther south. On market days farmers from beyond Orchard Gate, fishers from the docks, and travelers from the Coastal and Galross roads all converge. News spreads fast, and the well-worn stones of the central court serve as Dalraven's unofficial forum.</p>

</details>

<details id="guard-hall">
<summary><strong>Guard Hall</strong> — barracks, armory, and muster hall</summary>

<p>The Guard Hall sits on a side street behind the Moot Hall and across from Grey Ghost Hammersmiths, a squat stone building made for durability, not looks, with thick walls, narrow windows, and doors barred at night. Many guards live in town and newly hired blades are usually billeted at the Riverwake Inn, but the hall has a modest barracks chamber with cots for the guards on rotation, overnight watch, or disciplinary hold.</p>

<p>The front chamber is the muster hall, where notices are posted, patrol routes assigned, and disputes reported, with weapon racks of spears, halberds, crossbows, and shields in the burgomeister's livery. The armory at the back holds heavier emergency gear and a small, secure holding cell for short-term detention before transfer to the gaol or a hearing. An open training yard behind the building is used for drills and formation practice. With the docks and smithy so close, steel and authority stand side by side in Dalraven.</p>

</details>

<details id="moot-hall">
<summary><strong>Dalraven Moot Hall</strong> — the civic center and bell tower</summary>

<p>The Moot Hall rises above the rooftops with its square bell tower, the town's civic center and administrative seat. It is stone below and timber-framed above, dignified without excess, and stands in clear view of the docks and the main street. The ground floor holds a broad assembly chamber for town meetings, guild petitions, tax hearings, and public announcements, with benches facing a dais where the burgomeister or appointed officers preside.</p>

<p>Adjoining rooms include the tax reception office, where levies from farms, fisheries, and trade are recorded and counted, a records room holding property claims, writs, and ledgers, and a reinforced vault where coin waits before going south. A stair descends to the stone cellar, where barred cells form the town gaol. The second floor holds the offices of the burgomeister's clerks. The bronze bell in the tower marks market hours, calls meetings, and sounds the alarm for fire or coastal threats. Within the Moot Hall are kept Dalraven's coin, its law, and its memory.</p>

</details>

<details id="crown-garrison">
<summary><strong>Crown Garrison</strong> — the royal outpost</summary>

<p>The Crown Garrison is a fortified compound about one hundred fifty by eighty feet, built against the northern curtain wall. Three sides are closed by a twelve-foot stone wall and the fourth is the town's palisade. A heavy reinforced gate faces the town, wide enough for mounted cavalry to ride out two abreast. Inside, a substantial stone building holds the barracks, offices, records room, and a pair of holding cells, while the eastern half of the compound holds a packed-earth training yard and a large wooden stable for the cavalry mounts.</p>

<p>The garrison normally has two platoons of Algar Crown Cavaliers under a captain, with a lieutenant aide, a clerk, and four infantry on gate and compound duty. In practice at least one platoon is usually away on patrol, escort, or ducal assignment, and sometimes both are. The compound has no tower of its own, but a stair from the barracks roof can connect, with civic permission, to the North Gate watchtower. The garrison reminds everyone passing Dalraven's gates that the duchy, however wild and thinly settled, is under royal authority.</p>

</details>

<details id="irontrail-outfitters">
<summary><strong>Irontrail Outfitters</strong> — supplies for the road beyond the walls</summary>

<p>Irontrail Outfitters stands along the bridge road across from the Gull &amp; Gaff's rear stable yard and supplies those heading out beyond the safety of the walls. A broad wooden storefront shows coiled rope, bundled canvas, and weathered snowshoes on iron hooks, under a painted sign of a lantern swinging above a winding trail. Inside are shelves of lantern oil, bedrolls, trail rations, waxed map cases, and sturdy packs, with iron traps and snares hanging overhead and crates of pitons, grappling hooks, and torches by the counter. A locked cabinet holds compasses, sealed charts, and better mapping tools.</p>

<p>One corner is set aside for trade, where hunters and trappers bring pelts, hides, bone, and curiosities from the moors and nearby wilds, and prices follow the season and the quality. The proprietor, Merrik Thornfield, is a lean, middle-aged human and a member of the Merchant's Exchange. He speaks plainly about the risks of any road, neither glamorizing nor discouraging adventure, and makes sure customers understand what they lack before they leave.</p>

</details>

<details id="riverwake-inn">
<summary><strong>Riverwake Inn</strong> — the largest inn, by the bridge</summary>

<p>The Riverwake Inn backs onto the mouth of the Raven River where it meets Godsfall Bay and is Dalraven's largest and most prominent inn. It is three stories of heavy timber over a stone foundation, standing just inland from the bridge with its rear yard opening toward river and sea. A broad seasonal patio behind the common room is strung with lanterns and lined with low railings, with a view of the bridge, the slow river, and small craft moored at a private dock.</p>

<p>Rates are moderate and consistent, and the fare is dependable rather than fancy. Fishermen, traders, and road travelers mix freely in the common room. By long-standing arrangement with the burgomeister, most of the top floor is leased to non-local town guards and hired blades, which gives the inn steady income and a constant armed presence. The patio is favored by off-duty guards and mercenaries in good weather, and news travels fast there.</p>

</details>

<details id="petal-and-pestle">
<summary><strong>Petal &amp; Pestle</strong> — herbalist and greenhouse</summary>

<p>Across the road from the Riverwake Inn, Petal &amp; Pestle is both home and shop, a timber-framed building on a low stone foundation wrapped in climbing ivy and blossoms. A glass-roofed greenhouse extends from the back, its panes fogged and its air fragrant with unfamiliar growth. Pots, drying racks, bundled herbs, and hanging roots fill nearly every space inside and out.</p>

<p>Mistress Elowen "Wick" Bramblegear, a gnome herbalist of boundless curiosity and sharp wit, runs the shop and is known as much for her humor as her skill, often talking half to customers and half to the plants. She takes keen interest in travelers carrying anything not grown in Dalraven's soil and likes to barter for rare herbs, fungi, or seeds gathered beyond the duchy. The shop supplies poultices, tinctures, remedies, and basic alchemical components, and minor healing is available for a price, though Wick prefers a botanically interesting trade.</p>

</details>

<details id="blackwind-ciderworks">
<summary><strong>Blackwind Ciderworks</strong> — the windmill by the sea</summary>

<p>The tall coastal windmill within the walls is dedicated to fruit rather than grain. Blackwind Ciderworks uses its wide sails, turning in the sea breeze, to power a press that handles the town's orchard harvest each autumn. Apples from surrounding farms are crushed, pressed, and barreled here, and the juice ferments in cool stone cellars under the mill. Master Calder Fenwick, a practical, sharp-eyed member of the Brewers' &amp; Grocers' Compact, runs the business and also oversees distillation of stronger apple spirits and preparation of spiced preserves and vinegars.</p>

<p>Blackwind cider has modest renown along this stretch of coast. Barrels go north to Millesh Bay and south by sea to Bridgeport, where taverns sell it as a hardy frontier vintage. It is not refined enough for noble tables, but is respected for its strength and consistency. The mill is both industry and landmark, its turning sails visible from the sea and signaling to coastal vessels that Dalraven lies beyond the mudflats.</p>

</details>

<details id="ravenbank-fulling-mill">
<summary><strong>Ravenbank Fulling Mill</strong> — water-driven cloth finishing</summary>

<p>The Ravenbank Fulling Mill stands on the Raven River just beyond the bridge and uses the steady current to drive a row of heavy wooden stocks in its stone lower floor. These water-powered hammers pound woven wool cloth to clean, thicken, and strengthen it before it is sold or shipped. Raw fleeces from nearby farms are carded and woven in homes and small workshops and then brought here for finishing. The yard is often strung with cloth drying on timber frames, and the thud of the stocks carries along the riverbanks from dawn to dusk.</p>

<p>Stehl Ironweft, a broad-shouldered dwarf with iron-grey braids and a carrying voice, runs the mill. She is practical, unsentimental, and fiercely protective of quality, and she is a ranking member of the Clothiers' &amp; Fullers' Consortium, making sure guild marks are affixed before goods leave town. The mill produces durable wool for cloaks, blankets, sail reinforcement, and winter clothes bound north to Millesh Bay or south through the mountain roads. In lean fishing seasons its steady labor is a reliable income for many households.</p>

</details>

<details id="vaelthas-seaforth">
<summary><strong>Vaelthas Seaforth</strong> — maritime outfitter on the river</summary>

<p>Vaelthas Seaforth is a maritime outfitter on the Raven River near where it meets the docks, working from a long timber warehouse flanked by net frames, barrel stacks, and coiled rope. The yard serves Dalraven's fishing fleet and small coastal traders with barrels, rigging, mast fittings, sail repairs, and net work.</p>

<p>The proprietor, Caelirion Vaelthas, is an elf originally from Silmar, a former shipwright and woodsinger who once served in Silmar's naval defense. Here he applies his craft to humbler work. His barrels hold firm, his rigging is balanced, and his judgment of timber rivals any miller's. He can arrange the rental of a small single-masted riverboat for travel upstream, though he is selective about whom he trusts with one. He is measured, observant, and slow to give an opinion, and those who respect workmanship and restraint may find him a valuable ally.</p>

</details>


## Faith

<details id="house-of-bounty">
<summary><strong>House of Bounty</strong> — temple of Charr, the largest building in town</summary>

<p>The House of Bounty, temple of Charr, Eternal of Birth, Fertility, Nature, and Agriculture, is the largest single structure inside the walls. It is built of timber and pale stone quarried from the Algar Mountains, with a high-arched roof and broad front steps, and it serves as the town's living heart more than as an isolated sanctuary. Charr's symbol, a sheaf of wheat bound with a living vine, is carved above the main doors and repeated throughout the interior. The central hall is wide and open, built for gatherings as readily as devotions, so holy days fill it with hymns and ordinary evenings turn it into a meeting hall, dance floor, and refuge.</p>

<p>Alcoves along the side walls hold shrines to Charr's Ascendants: Sivet the Hearthmother, honored with candles and woven cradle charms; Olya Greenbriar, marked by potted herbs and trellised vines; and Barrowen Tallgrain, Ascendant of Harvest and Stewardship, shown with carved grain heads and small offerings of seed. Mature trees shade the grounds and a broad wagon turnabout used for festivals and harvest gatherings, where farmers, fishers, dockworkers, and townsfolk feast, dance, and give thanks. Across the yard stands a manse for the prior and the temple custodian, both trained midwives and healers, which also lodges visiting priests and holy warriors of Charr's orders.</p>

</details>

<details id="shrine-of-the-deep-current">
<summary><strong>Shrine of the Deep Current</strong> — shrine of Cora, Primordial of Water</summary>

<p>Near the eastern edge of the walls stands the Shrine of the Deep Current, dedicated to Cora, Primordial of Water. It is set well back from the shoreline now, though a street runs west from it directly to the docks. It was not always inland. The shrine once stood at the water's edge, where the surf struck its stones directly, until a violent storm and flood shattered it. Its carved stones were salvaged and reassembled within the walls, and the central altar still bears scars from that restoration, a reminder that sea and town do not always agree.</p>

<p>Unlike the ordered House of Bounty, this shrine is simple and windworn, a ring of weathered stones around a tide-carved altar stained by brine and wax. Offerings are practical rather than ornate. A pair of Tidewardens, experienced mariners, tend the stones and oversee quiet observances, and a senior Wavemaster conducts departure rites, storm supplications, and memorials for those lost at sea. No sermons are given and no doctrine is debated. The sea does not require theology.</p>

</details>


## Beyond the Walls

<details id="ravenbridge">
<summary><strong>Ravenbridge</strong> — the trestle bridge over the Raven River</summary>

<p>A broad wooden trestle bridge spans the Raven River and connects Dalraven's southern fringe and farmlands to the walled town. It is built of heavy timber beams reinforced with iron straps and is wide enough for wagons, though two heavily laden carts must slow down to pass each other safely.</p>

<p>The trestle supports descend into the river at regular, close-set intervals, so only small craft such as rowboats and fishing skiffs pass between them easily. A single-masted coastal longboat might clear the span if its mast were short or lowered, but few attempt it unless they must.</p>

</details>

<details id="ashborne-estate">
<summary><strong>Ashborne Estate</strong> — old farmland south of the river</summary>

<p>South of the Raven River, beyond Orchard Gate, lies Ashborne Estate, one of the oldest and most extensive landholdings in the Duchy of Dalraven. Its fields produce grain and hardy vegetables, its orchards supply apples for local pressing, and its livestock graze upland pastures that rise toward the mountains. An inland windmill grinds grain for the estate.</p>

<p>Dame Elidra Ashborne, a retired officer of the Algarian Crown who once commanded a coastal defense company, oversees it. Her armor hangs in the great hall now, but her bearing is still plainly martial. The household keeps disciplined hands and a modest but capable armed staff, which is necessary where beasts and worse sometimes come down from the high country. The estate keeps a private dock on the river with access to Dalraven's wharves, an independence that has earned it both respect and quiet resentment. Dame Elidra judges character quickly. Those who prove honorable and capable may find rest, work, or information here, and those who posture without substance are sent back across the bridge.</p>

</details>

<details id="vayne-manor">
<summary><strong>Vayne Manor</strong> — the burgomeister's residence</summary>

<p>Vayne Manor stands on a rise overlooking the River Road and is the residence of Burgomeister Halric Vayne. Its marble-faced main house contrasts sharply with the practical timber and stone buildings that make up most of Dalraven. It is not ostentatious by capital standards, but it is plainly the grandest private residence in the area.</p>

<p>The manor sits within a heavily treed park that extends across the River Road to the riverbank, with carefully kept paths through groves of imported trees and ornamental plantings and a private dock at the water's edge for discreet arrival by boat. A spacious guest house stands near the main house at the end of a broad gravel drive and hosts visiting officials, notable merchants, and sometimes Crown representatives. Barns, paddocks, and cultivated fields lie north and west of the park, underscoring the Vayne family's status as major landowners in the duchy.</p>

</details>
