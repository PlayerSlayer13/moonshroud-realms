# Funnis Locations

The roads, halls, gates, and wild places in and around Funnis, in the order a traveler would meet them as they arrive from the road. Click any entry to expand it. ## Approaching Funnis

<details id="brackle-road">
<summary><strong>Brackle Road</strong> — the only road in and out</summary>

<p>Brackle Road is a hard-packed dirt track and Funnis's one real link to the outside world. It follows Clear Creek through the lower Sanctum Hills, wide enough for two wagons to pass for most of its length but narrower and rougher the further it goes. North of the settlement it climbs steeply toward the Livingstone Mountains and gradually wears down to a walking trail that wagons cannot use. Only the bold or the desperate attempt that stretch, which locals call the High Pass.</p>

<p>The Ursinor maintain the road through their own territory. A royal inspector walks it once a year from Brackle, and the Crown handles serious washouts and structural repairs.</p>

</details>

<details id="funnis-bridge">
<summary><strong>Funnis Bridge</strong> — plain to look at, remarkable to cross</summary>

<p>At first glance the bridge is nothing special: a wooden beam span laid across the creek's ravine, with no ornament. Look closer and the Ursinor skill shows. The joints are tight, the weight is spread so evenly that heavy loads and high winds barely make it creak, and wagons crossing at speed raise only a dull hum. Rain drains off without pooling, so the timbers do not rot. It stands high above the ravine and holds together without iron fittings or visible bracing.</p>

<p>Residents trust it in every season, and visitors often get their first lesson in Ursinor woodworking from it. A watchtower at the village end lets sentries see down the road and into the ravine, so they can signal the Main Gate before anyone arrives.</p>

</details>

<details id="wagon-yard">
<summary><strong>Wagon Yard</strong> — where carts wait to be looked over</summary>

<p>Just past the bridge lies a broad yard of packed earth, about a hundred feet across and firm even after heavy rain. Carts turn, unload, and wait for inspection here, and several can stand at once without crowding. To the southwest stand the lumber compound's stacked timbers and sheds, with the windmill's sails creaking overhead. Ahead rises the Main Gate, where two guards watch arrivals. Worn footpaths run along the outside of the palisade to the north and west, used by sentries, herders, and anyone moving between the towers without going through the gate.</p>

</details>

<details id="main-gate">
<summary><strong>Main Gate</strong> — a two-story gatehouse in the palisade</summary>

<p>The Main Gate is a solid two-story timber gatehouse with a covered walkway on top where archers keep watch. Below it, heavy double gates are wide enough for wagons, and a smaller door set into them is used when the gates are shut. By day one leaf is usually left open for the steady traffic of workers, herders, and mill crews. Watchtowers at the nearest palisade corners give overlapping views across the yard and the bridge, and one or two guards at ground level calmly challenge anyone they do not recognize.</p>

</details>

<details id="sawmill">
<summary><strong>Sawmill</strong> — the lumber compound outside the walls</summary>

<p>The sawmill sits just outside the palisade, inside its own low fence, and the yard smells of sap, wet bark, and fresh-cut wood. Raw logs are stacked along the outer edge beside waiting wagons and crates, while the middle of the compound holds sawn planks, sheds, carts, and covered warehouses around a tall windmill whose turning sails drive the machinery. A small round hut near the river serves for tool storage, watch duty, and tracking water levels. A watchtower at the far end looks over the forest beyond Whistling Run and the logging trails.</p>

<p>Ursinor workers labor here all day under a seasoned guild master, who also steers unfamiliar visitors toward the Wayhouse before their curiosity becomes a distraction.</p>

</details>

## Within the Walls

<details id="wayhouse">
<summary><strong>Wayhouse</strong> — the settlement's front desk</summary>

<p>The Wayhouse stands just inside the Main Gate and is the first stop for strangers, known traders, returning travelers, and messengers. Its work is practical rather than ceremonial. Names are recorded, intentions noted, and visitors quietly assessed before they are welcomed further or sent elsewhere. Stewards, scribes, and trusted elders staff it in rotating shifts.</p>

<p>It also serves as the village's everyday administrative hub. Petitions to the Council Lodge, requests for trade approval, reports of trouble on the road or in the woods, and small disputes all arrive here first and are logged and passed to the proper body. It has no power of judgment or decree, only coordination and memory. For a traveler it is the safest place to ask questions, arrange a meeting, or learn where it is polite to go. On the rare occasions Funnis needs help from outside its own clans, the request is made here, with the task, the expectations, and the authority behind it set out plainly.</p>

</details>

<details id="gate-lodge">
<summary><strong>Gate Lodge</strong> — hospitality with a watchful eye</summary>

<p>The Gate Lodge sits just inside the palisade near the Main Gate. It is low and broad, built of timber and stone, with thick walls and deep eaves that echo the longhouses without imitating them. That is the clue that it is meant for guests rather than kin. A wide hearth anchors the common room and burns through most of the year, and there are heavy benches, racks for drying cloaks, and pegs sized for travelers of every build. Simple sleeping alcoves line the back, private enough but not comfortable enough to invite a long stay.</p>

<p>No one pays for a bed here. Outsiders are rare, and those allowed inside the walls are either in need of brief shelter or present at the community's invitation. Appointed wardens look after guests quietly, making sure they are safe, treated with respect, and understood. During moots and gatherings the lodge usually houses Ursinor from other communities. It is not an inn. It is a place to rest, be known, and move on.</p>

</details>

<details id="first-voice-hall">
<summary><strong>First Voice Hall</strong> — for matters of consequence</summary>

<p>The First Voice Hall is a public-facing building kept for weighty business rather than daily governance. Here the First Voice speaks for the clans, receives formal delegations, and presides over mediation or judgment when consensus cannot be reached elsewhere. Lasting announcements, ceremonial welcomes, and the rare matter involving outsiders that affects the whole settlement are all handled here. Anyone bringing a matter before the hall is expected to arrive prepared, with their position clear and the consequences acknowledged. Proceedings are infrequent and deliberate, and the hall has a quiet gravity because of it.</p>

<p>At the far end of the chamber a huge, elaborately carved seat stands on a raised dais, decorated with layered forest scenes, flowing grain, and stylized beasts of the surrounding wilds. It is not a throne. It is a reminder that the First Voice speaks with collective trust and not personal authority. Behind the ceremonial space are private chambers, including the First Voice's home, rooms for confidential talks, and quarters for attendants, all tightly guarded.</p>

</details>

<details id="market">
<summary><strong>Market</strong> — a barter ground for extras</summary>

<p>This is not a bazaar and not a place where outside coin changes hands. Staples such as grain, lumber, wool, and preserved food go straight from the barns and storehouses to the longhouses, so the market is left for whatever is extra: fine woodwork, decorative tools, carved tokens, cured hides, and small luxuries saved for festivals. A herbalist might lay out a rare plant gathered deep in the woods, a tanner a single exceptional pelt, a craftsperson a weapon or tool made with unusual care. Shamans and elders sometimes offer charms or small blessings, quietly and without advertising.</p>

<p>Trade is by barter, with coin accepted only rarely. The market swells before celebrations and nearly empties during hard work weeks. Notices about trade routes, seasonal restrictions, and guild expectations are posted here, and appointed stewards keep records of outgoing shipments.</p>

</details>

<details id="council-lodge">
<summary><strong>Council Lodge</strong> — where the clan leaders meet</summary>

<p>The Council Lodge is the quiet center of Funnis's internal government. Each of the six clans has a carved seat or standing marker inside, set in a broad circle so that no clan sits above another. The Axe Beak, Blink Dog, Dire Wolf, Giant Owl, Owlbear, and Wyvern clans are all represented. Matters brought here are deliberate and infrequent, from seasonal planning and trade concerns to quarrels between clans and questions touching the whole community. No votes are taken. Talk continues until agreement is found or the matter is set aside. The lodge stands empty more often than not, but when its doors close, what is decided inside carries lasting weight.</p>

</details>

<details id="trade-hall">
<summary><strong>Trade Hall</strong> — the public face of Funnis commerce</summary>

<p>Among the buildings inside the walls, only the Wayhouse, the Gate Lodge, and the market are easier for an outsider to enter than the Trade Hall. It is where recognized guild and trade business is done, so that outsiders can handle legitimate affairs without stepping into more private communal spaces. Guild representatives, factors, and known merchants are received here to discuss contracts, delivery schedules, standards, and dues for timber, wool, and select crafted goods.</p>

<p>On rare occasions, when a problem is beyond the clans' capacity or interest, sanctioned guild work may be offered through the hall. Such tasks are never casual, and they are only offered with the knowledge of the Council Lodge, so that any outside involvement stays controlled and accountable.</p>

</details>

<details id="common-hall">
<summary><strong>Common Hall</strong> — the social heart of the village</summary>

<p>The Common Hall is where the clans eat, celebrate, and gather every day. Unlike the Council Lodge or the First Voice Hall, it is informal and welcoming, with long tables and open hearths set out to encourage conversation over ceremony. Notices about daily life are read aloud here, and minor or personal disputes are often talked through before they ever reach clan leaders. Seasonal feasts, naming celebrations, and rites for planting, harvest, and successful hunts are all held here, tying the longhouses together.</p>

<p>Visitors who have been allowed past the gate are occasionally invited in, a rare and meaningful gesture of trust. More than any other building, the Common Hall shows what Funnis values: shared labor, shared food, and the belief that a community holds together through familiarity and mutual obligation rather than authority.</p>

</details>

<details id="barns">
<summary><strong>Barns</strong> — storehouses spread across the settlement</summary>

<p>Funnis does not keep its barns in one yard. A central cluster near the longhouses holds the shared staples, including grain, dried food, wool, and essential tools, and provisions are drawn from it and handed out to the clans as part of the regular distribution. More barns and storehouses stand apart, mostly near the corrals and along the outer paths. These handle processing and staging: harvest yields, sheared wool, surplus goods, and materials waiting for trade or further work. Activity around them rises and falls with the seasons, with shearing, harvest, and winter preparation making the outlying barns especially busy.</p>

</details>

<details id="longhouses">
<summary><strong>Longhouses</strong> — clan homes and gathering places</summary>

<p>The longhouses are the core of residential life and clan identity. Each of the six clans keeps at least one principal longhouse, which serves as both a home and a place for the clan's own gatherings. They vary in size and layout to suit the traditions of whoever lives in them. The clans are not equal in number, and the buildings show it. Larger or busier clans keep extra longhouses for extended families, returning travelers, and seasonal workers, while smaller clans pool their households as needed. Over time a longhouse may change hands or be put to a new use, which lets the village adapt without abandoning its traditions.</p>

</details>

<details id="corrals">
<summary><strong>Corrals</strong> — sheep, goats, and chickens</summary>

<p>The corrals hold the settlement's livestock, mainly sheep raised for wool and goats kept for milk, cheese, and the occasional meal. Herding is shared, with duties rotating among the clans through the seasons. The pens have simple but effective weather shelters, feeding racks, and gates that let the animals be separated for shearing, kidding, or illness. Chicken coops and open runs sit just outside the main pens, and the birds roam freely by day, which keeps insects down. Early mornings and evenings are crowded with herders, children, and workers, making this one of the busiest parts of Funnis apart from the market.</p>

</details>

<details id="north-gate">
<summary><strong>North Gate</strong> — the working gate</summary>

<p>The North Gate is a practical, lightly fortified exit used almost entirely by the people of Funnis, not by visiting traffic. It opens toward the working lands, hunting paths, and river access that feed the community, and it is rarely barred during the day. A steady stream of herders, hunters, foragers, and fishers passes through. The gate is a stout timber structure wide enough for carts but mostly used by people on foot or leading animals, and the ground around it is worn smooth from generations of use. Trail posts radiate outward to the fields, grazing grounds, river bends, and safer woodland routes, and the community keeps them updated as conditions change.</p>

<p>A small guard detail is usually present, more to watch and signal than to control the gate. In bad weather or tense times the gate may be closed at dusk, but normally it reflects how much Funnis trusts its routines and the land.</p>

</details>

## Outside the Walls

<details id="watchtowers">
<summary><strong>Watchtowers</strong> — built for archers and early warning</summary>

<p>The Woodlands Ursinor are famed for their archery, and the towers show it. They are placed with care along the palisade to make the most of height, sightlines, and the lie of the land, and they offer stable firing platforms, covered positions, and clear views instead of heavy fortification. Beyond the walls, larger standalone platforms watch the key approaches, including the bridge, the nearby fields, and stretches of the river. These outlying towers are early warning posts, able to spot approaching travelers, unfamiliar wagons, prowling beasts, or bandit bands long before they arrive. Signals pass quickly to the gates and the inner watch, so Funnis is rarely surprised even though it keeps no standing army.</p>

</details>

<details id="boat-launch">
<summary><strong>Boat Launch</strong> — steps down to the creek</summary>

<p>A flight of stone-cut steps descends the ravine to a calmer stretch of Clear Creek. Simple dugouts are pulled ashore or tied to posts there, used for fishing, checking nets, or short river trips when the paths are impassable. The launch is narrow and deliberately understated, which discourages heavy traffic but keeps it usable all year. In high water the steps give access to the creek without using the bridge, and in winter crews take turns clearing them of ice.</p>

<p>Because the launch lies outside the palisade, the watchtowers keep an eye on it constantly. Outsiders are rarely allowed to use the boats without an escort, though trusted messengers and clan crews depend on them often. That makes the launch both a practical necessity and a quiet checkpoint on who comes and goes.</p>

</details>

<details id="woods">
<summary><strong>Woods</strong> — the forest around Funnis</summary>

<p>The Whispering Woods cover the rolling uplands of the Sanctum Hills, a temperate forest shaped by wind, water, and long stewardship. Oak, beech, and duskwood dominate the lower reaches, their canopies dimming the forest floor over ferns, mosses, mushrooms, and hardy shrubs. Clear Creek and its tributaries cut winding ravines through the trees, feeding quiet pools and stony runs full of fish, frogs, and waterfowl. The name comes from the near-constant rustle of leaves and boughs, a sound that carries strangely and often unsettles newcomers.</p>

<p>On the northern slopes, where the land rises toward the Livingstone Mountains, pine begins to mix with the hardwoods, and the groves there are cooler, darker, and windier. Scattered through the forest are rare moonheart trees, a pale-green hardwood that carpenters and bowyers prize for its strength and resonance. The Ursinor manage them carefully and never fell one lightly, so fallen limbs are gathered far more often than trunks.</p>

<p>Game is plentiful, including deer, boar, foxes, black bears, hares, badgers, and wild fowl, along with wolves, giant owls, and the occasional owlbear wandering down from the hills. Deeper in, giant spiders and aggressive plants are known. Bandits and smugglers sometimes try these paths to slip around Funnis, though few linger before they are noticed. North of the treeline the forest thins into broken hills and mountain approaches. Giants are rumored to live deeper in the Livingstone Mountains, but sightings in the woods themselves are rare, spoken of mostly in cautious whispers, and are one reason people avoid the High Pass.</p>

</details>
